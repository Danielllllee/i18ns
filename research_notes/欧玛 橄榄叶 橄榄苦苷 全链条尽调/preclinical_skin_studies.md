# Preclinical skin evidence for olive (Olea europaea) leaf extract (OLE), oleuropein and related olive phenolics: skin inflammation, UV-induced redness and skin repair (dossier for evaluating a cosmetic anti-redness claim)

Method note for the report writer: every item below was checked **only through WebSearch result extracts** (abstract-level text, figure captions, repository records and some secondary reviews). Direct page fetching failed (WebFetch returned `getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND` for both nature.com and pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), so **no full text was read**. Exact effect sizes, statistics, funding statements and methods details are therefore often missing and are listed as gaps. Where a number comes from a secondary source (e.g., the MDedge/The Hospitalist "Cosmeceutical Critique" review) rather than the primary abstract, it is flagged. Abbreviations: OLE = olive leaf extract; HT = hydroxytyrosol; Verb = verbascoside. Conversions use oleuropein MW 540.51 g/mol ([Sigma-Aldrich listing](https://b2bqws.sigmaaldrich.com/KR/en/substance/oleuropein5405132619424)): 1 µg/mL oleuropein ≈ 1.85 µM; 10 µM ≈ 5.4 µg/mL.

## Key Question 1 — Skin cell studies: which markers changed, with which test material, at what concentrations

### Takeaway
The skin-cell evidence is thin and scattered. The redness-relevant inflammatory markers (COX-2, IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, NF-κB p65, p38/ERK MAPK) after UVB come almost entirely from one 2025 Scientific Reports paper that used **pure** oleuropein, HT and verbascoside (20 µM each alone, 10+10+10 µM combined, UVB 80 mJ/cm²), where single compounds had only partial effects. The only fibroblast study of an extract with a stated oleuropein content (Machała 2022: 22.2% oleuropein, 2.5–25 µg/mL, roughly 1–10 µM oleuropein-equivalent, UVA 8 J/cm²) measured mainly oxidative stress, DNA damage and apoptosis. An earlier olive-leaf/UVA fibroblast study found **no significant ROS reduction** for most olive extracts. Results on melanocytes conflict, and one paper reports oleuropein cytotoxicity at high doses.

### Cited Findings

**Dermal fibroblasts (Hs68, NHDF, HDF, HDFa)**
- **Machała et al., Molecules 2022;27(16):5144 (DOI 10.3390/molecules27165144; University of Łódź group: Machała, Liudvytska, Kicel, Dziedzic, Olszewska, Żbikowska).** Test material: LC-PDA-MS/MS-standardized ethanol–water OLE, **26.2% total phenols, 22.2% oleuropein**. Model: Hs68 human skin fibroblasts pretreated 24 h with **OLE 2.5–25 µg/mL**, then UVA **8 J/cm²**. Comparators: quercetin and ascorbic acid at 25 µg/mL. Results: OLE significantly reduced UVA-induced DNA damage and ROS overproduction, increased thioredoxin reductase (TrxR) expression and post-irradiation viability by inhibiting apoptosis. Both intrinsic and extrinsic apoptotic pathways appeared inhibited, with caspase 9 activity reduced most. — [PMC9415354](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9415354/); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1420-3049/27/16/5144)
- Machała 2022: the TrxR figure shows OLE at 5 and 25 µg/mL alongside the UVA dose. — [PMC figure f006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9415354/figure/molecules-27-05144-f006)
- Machała 2022: in a separate **human lymphocyte** culture (not skin cells), OLE significantly decreased UVA-induced secretion of TNF-α and IL-2. The authors propose OLE as a topical photoprotective, antioxidant and anti-inflammatory agent; this is their interpretation of in vitro data. — [PMC9415354](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9415354/)
- **Contradicting earlier study: Czerwińska, Duszak, Parzonko, Kiss, Acta Biol Cracov Ser Bot 2016;58(2) (DOI 10.1515/abcsb-2016-0016).** Aqueous and ethanolic olive and privet (Ligustrum vulgare) leaf extracts in UVA-irradiated **NHDF**. Most extracts did **not** significantly reduce UVA-induced ROS; the only exception was aqueous privet extract at 5 µg/mL. Aqueous extracts of both species at 5 and 25 µg/mL protected viability; ethanolic extracts did not. No significant difference between olive and privet. — [Journal PDF](https://journals.pan.pl//Content/103247/PDF/ABC%20Bot.%202-16%204-M.Czerwinska%20i%20in.pdf); [AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/65dfd4277c7033e84bef1c0b)
- **Gayatri … Shimizu, Records of Natural Products 2025;19(5):596–610 (DOI 10.25135/rnp.544.2506.3557; Kyushu University with Egyptian co-authors).** Oleuropein was isolated from olive leaves and tested in NHDF from adult donors. At **100–200 µg/mL (≈185–370 µM)** it promoted proliferation above 120%. At **200 µg/mL** it protected against UVB **250 mJ/cm²**, with viability maintained at **76.54 ± 5.05%**. The UVB-only control value was not in the extract. No inflammatory markers were reported in the extracts. — [Journal page](https://www.acgpubs.org/article/records-of-natural-products/2025/5-september-october/the-etoac-fraction-from-the-olive-leaves-showed-promoting-cell-growth-and-skin-protectant-against-uvb-in-nhdf-cells-and-melanogenesis-inhibitory-in-b16-melanoma-cells-for-the-first-time); [PDF](https://acgpubs.org/doc/20250821153635A2-544-RNP-2506-3557.pdf)
- **Li et al., Int J Food Sci Nutr 2022;73(4) (URI group; Navindra Seeram co-author).** Pure oleuropein and HT showed moderate anti-elastase and anti-collagenase activity at **62.5–1000 µM**, with synergistic elastase inhibition at a 1:1 ratio. HT reduced H2O2-induced cytotoxicity and ROS in human dermal fibroblasts. Oleuropein/HT combinations at **6.25/6.25–100/100 µM** were synergistically cytoprotective, cutting ROS by **7.6–37.3%** (combination index 0.17–0.44). The title calls the compounds "skin permeable", but no permeation data appeared in the extracts. — [URI Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/bps_facpubs/331)
- **Int J Clin Pract 2024 (DOI 10.1155/2024/8827501)** on pure oleuropein in normal skin cells, using MTT, flow cytometry, DAPI, DPPH/ABTS, scratch assay and qRT-PCR. The abstract reports oleuropein was **cytotoxic to skin cells at higher exposure doses**, induced apoptosis on flow cytometry, and had "strong anti-inflammation and antioxidant" effects. Doses were not in the extract. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/8af94024771b4d629a631595c4aad7a7)
- **2026 HDFa study (journal not confirmed; repository/mirror page appears to reference J Craniofac Surg).** Oleuropein **10–200 µM** had no discernible effect on human dermal fibroblast viability (MTT). This conflicts with the 2024 cytotoxicity report, possibly because of dose ranges. — [Medipol mirror page](https://sabita.medipol.edu.tr/index.php/2026/06/24/article-the-journal-of-craniofacial-surgery-3/)
- **Hydroxytyrosol in HDF (Biomed Dermatol 2018, DOI 10.1186/s41702-018-0031-x).** The figure captions state that IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8 and MMP-1 mRNA "increase with the hydroxytyrosol concentration", while a supplier marketing page describing the same work says they decrease. The direction is **unresolved** from the available text. — [Figure 3](https://biomeddermatol.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s41702-018-0031-x/figures/3); [Figure 4](https://biomeddermatol.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s41702-018-0031-x/figures/4); [supplier page (promotional)](https://www.knowde.com/stores/atanor/products/olivan-antiox-liquid)

**Keratinocytes (HaCaT; primary human keratinocytes) and keratinocyte–fibroblast co-culture**
- **Scientific Reports 2025;15:41008, "Combined protection against UVB-induced photoaging by oleuropein, hydroxytyrosol, and verbascoside…" (DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-24845-4; PMID 41266576; PMC12635131; published 20 Nov 2025).** Extracts disagree on author order: PubMed lists Jing Wang et al., and the publisher page lists Minjia Yuan and Chenye Shen as first authors. Test material: **pure compounds**, not an extract. Each was used alone at **20 µM**; the combination (O/H/V) was **10 µM oleuropein + 10 µM HT + 10 µM Verb (1:1:1)**. UVB was **80 mJ/cm²**. Models: HaCaT, HDF, and a HaCaT–HDF transwell co-culture. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-24845-4); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12635131/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41266576/)
- Sci Rep 2025, single compounds in keratinocytes: each lowered UVB-induced c-Fos, c-Jun, TNF-α and IL-1β expression and partly restored Col1a mRNA. Oleuropein and Verb, but **not HT**, significantly reduced MMP1 and IL-6 gene expression. At the **protein** level all three reduced UVB-induced IL-6 secretion but had **no appreciable effect on MMP1 or Col1a protein**. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-24845-4)
- Sci Rep 2025, combination: reduced UVB-induced ROS accumulation and oxidative DNA damage, restored Nrf2 at mRNA and protein level, inhibited phosphorylation of p38, ERK2 and NF-κB p65, and reduced COX-2. The authors write that individual compounds "had less effects". In the co-culture, the combination applied to UVB-irradiated HaCaT significantly reduced pro-inflammatory and collagen-degradation genes in HDFs and restored Col1a and Col3a. — [PMC12635131](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12635131/); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-24845-4)
- **Li et al., Front Pharmacol, published 8 Jan 2025 (DOI 10.3389/fphar.2024.1496078).** Pure oleuropein at **50 and 100 µM** reduced H2O2-induced HaCaT cell death by **6.4% and 9.2%**, which the authors attribute to lower ROS. Oleuropein also lowered IL-1β, inhibited caspase-1 (IL-1-converting enzyme) activity and ameliorated pyroptosis in HaCaT cells. IL-1β and thioredoxin-interacting protein were flagged as candidate targets. In **THP-1 monocytes** (not skin), oleuropein **12–200 µM** reduced IL-6 by **38.8–45.5%**. — [PMC11751055](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11751055/); [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2024.1496078/pdf)
- **Current Issues in Molecular Biology 2026;48:549 (published 23 May 2026), "Olive Leaf Extract Suppresses Sebogenesis and Inflammation via AKT/ERK and SREBP-1/PPAR-γ Signaling in Human Sebocytes".** Authors are from **Durae Corporation (industry)** and Eulji University. In HaCaT cells stimulated with Cutibacterium, OLE reduced **IL-8, TNF-α and PGE2**. Extract composition, oleuropein % and concentrations were not in the extracts. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/cimb48060549); [MDPI PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/cimb/cimb-48-00549/article_deploy/cimb-48-00549.pdf)
- **Hydroxytyrosol, Guo et al. 2010 (HaCaT, UVB).** HT reduced UVB-induced DNA strand breaks, intracellular ROS and 8-OHdG, and attenuated p53 and NF-κB expression concentration-dependently. Concentrations and the journal were not confirmed in the extract. — [AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/65de16597c7033e84be9b94a)
- **Hydroxytyrosol, Aparicio-Soto et al., Mol Nutr Food Res 2019;63:1900019 (primary human keratinocytes).** HT or HT-acetate pretreatment before IL-1β or a TLR3 ligand reduced TSLP protein dose-dependently and inhibited TSLP isoform and IL-8 mRNA. Mechanistically it blocked IκB degradation and NF-κB nuclear translocation and reduced NF-κB binding at the TSLP and IL-8 promoters. — [FU Berlin repository](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/handle/fub188/33517); [Univ. Sevilla repository](https://idus.us.es/handle/11441/128572)
- **Hydroxytyrosol, Rafehi et al., Genes Nutr 2011.** By mRNA-Seq, HT at **20 µM** in keratinocytes raised heme oxygenase-1 about **15-fold**, with smaller increases in glutaredoxin and glutathione peroxidase, and gave modest radioprotection. One figure legend says "20 mM", probably a typo. — [PMC3316757](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3316757); [Figure 5](https://genesandnutrition.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1007/s12263-011-0249-3/figures/5)
- **Hydroxytyrosol and olive phenols, Salucci/Burattini group (Bologna/Urbino).** Pre-treatment before UVB reduced apoptotic ultrastructural patterns, TUNEL-positive nuclei and caspase activation in HaCaT cells. The tyrosol paper is J Dermatol Sci 2015;80(1):61–68, and the HT data appear in a 2014 conference abstract. — [IJAE 2014 abstract](https://oajournals.fupress.net/index.php/ijae/article/view/2525); [Univ. Bologna record](https://cris.unibo.it/handle/11585/545367); [EJH article](https://ejh.it/ejh/article/download/2784/2705/17891)
- **Hydroxytyrosol, Abate et al. 2021.** HT stimulated keratinocyte proliferation at low micromolar concentrations, increased migration and protected against H2O2 injury. This is wound-repair-relevant but involved no UV or inflammatory readout. — [PMC7957601](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7957601/)

**Sebocytes**
- The only olive-leaf sebocyte study found is CIMB 2026 (industry-affiliated). It used **SEB-1** sebocytes, not SZ95. OLE inhibited linoleic acid-induced lipid accumulation without cytotoxicity, with SREBP-1 and PPAR-γ as mechanistic markers. — [MDPI PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/cimb/cimb-48-00549/article_deploy/cimb-48-00549.pdf)

**Melanocytes (results conflict)**
- **Cho et al., Int J Mol Sci 2024;25(8):4479.** Oleuropein, oleocanthal and oleacein were tested in B16F10 cells and human epidermal melanocytes (HEM). The authors report **pro-melanogenic** effects ("promoting melanin production"), with 5 µM chosen as a non-cytotoxic concentration. — [PMC11050296](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11050296/)
- Gayatri/Shimizu 2025 report the opposite: oleuropein at **100 µg/mL** reduced B16 melanin content to **35 ± 2.15%** without cytotoxicity. — [Journal page](https://www.acgpubs.org/article/records-of-natural-products/2025/5-september-october/the-etoac-fraction-from-the-olive-leaves-showed-promoting-cell-growth-and-skin-protectant-against-uvb-in-nhdf-cells-and-melanogenesis-inhibitory-in-b16-melanoma-cells-for-the-first-time)
- A 2025 MDPI paper on purified oleuropein in B16F10 and primary human melanocytes notes that the same authors' standardized OLE had increased extracellular melanin and dendricity. Per the search summary, it describes the oleuropein literature as inconsistent (pro- vs anti-melanogenic, depending on cell type and concentration). — [MDPI 2673-8449/5/2/8](https://mdpi.com/2673-8449/5/2/8)

**Mast cells (not skin-derived)**
- **Persia et al., Phytomedicine 2014;21(11):1400–1405 (DOI 10.1016/j.phymed.2014.05.010).** Purified **rat peritoneal** mast cells were preincubated with HT or oleuropein (dose-response arm **10, 50, 100, 200, 400 µM**; preincubation 5–45 min), then stimulated with compound 48/80, concanavalin A or calcium ionophore A23187. Degranulation was measured as β-hexosaminidase release, with sodium cromoglycate as comparator. Both compounds inhibited degranulation through immune and non-immune pathways. Percent-inhibition values were not in the extracts. — [CONICET repository](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/handle/11336/32037); [MINCyT record](https://repositoriosdigitales.mincyt.gob.ar/vufind/Record/CONICETDig_b5d453665b99b88c179758542078c27d); [IntechOpen chapter](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/66111)

**Endothelial cells (vascular, not skin microvascular)**
- **Scoditti et al., Arch Biochem Biophys 2012;527.** Cultured endothelial cells were pre-incubated with oleuropein, HT, resveratrol or quercetin at **0.1–50 µM**, then stimulated with PMA. All four reduced tube formation and migration. This tracked with lower COX-2 protein, less prostanoid production, less MMP-9 release and gelatinolytic activity, and less oxidative stress and NF-κB activation. — [CNR record](https://explore.cnr.it/resource/item/396889); [IRIS CNR](https://iris.cnr.it/handle/20.500.14243/298920)
- **Oncotarget 2017 (B16F10 melanoma allograft, dietary oleuropein).** Oleuropein directly inhibited tube formation by HUVECs and lymphatic endothelial cells and was linked to lower VEGF secretion. — [PMC5458266](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5458266/)

**Macrophages (inflammatory context, not skin)**
- **Ryu et al., J Agric Food Chem 2015;63(7):2098–2105.** In LPS-stimulated RAW 264.7 cells, oleuropein lowered NO by suppressing iNOS and COX-2, reduced IL-1β and IL-6, blocked IκB-α phosphorylation and p65 translocation, reduced ERK1/2 and JNK activation, and reduced NO in LPS-challenged zebrafish. — [AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65ded5cf4c5aef494fde342e)
- **Front Pharmacol 2021 (DOI 10.3389/fphar.2021.617314).** Oleuropein reduced NO, IL-6, TNF-α, iNOS and COX-2 in LPS macrophages, bound TLR4, inhibited TLR4 dimerization and TLR4–MyD88 binding, and alleviated LPS-induced acute kidney injury in mice. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2021.617314/full)
- **Contradictory: Visioli, Bellosta, Galli, Life Sci 1998;62(6):541–546.** In LPS-stimulated mouse macrophages, oleuropein **increased** nitrite output dose-dependently. The effect was reversed by L-NAME and accompanied by higher iNOS protein. — [Univ. Milan repository](https://air.unimi.it/handle/2434/208993); [Univ. Milan expertise](https://expertise.unimi.it/resource/item/218963)

### Inferences
- **Concentration conversions.** These are from the cited numbers and MW 540.51.
  - Machała OLE 2.5–25 µg/mL × 22.2% ≈ 0.56–5.6 µg/mL oleuropein ≈ **1.0–10.3 µM**.
  - Sci Rep 2025: 20 µM oleuropein ≈ 10.8 µg/mL; 10 µM ≈ 5.4 µg/mL.
  - Li 2025 HaCaT: 50–100 µM ≈ 27–54 µg/mL.
  - Persia mast cells: 10–400 µM ≈ 5.4–216 µg/mL.
  - Scoditti: 0.1–50 µM.
  - Gayatri 2025: 100–200 µg/mL ≈ 185–370 µM.
  - The redness-relevant cytokine and COX-2 effects were therefore seen at roughly 10–100 µM, and the fibroblast-protection effect at about 200 µg/mL.
- **Test material matters.** The best mechanistic data (Sci Rep 2025) use pure compounds, and synergy depended on verbascoside. An OLE ingredient cannot borrow these results unless its oleuropein, HT and verbascoside contents and ratio are shown to match. Only Machała 2022 used an extract with a stated oleuropein %, and its endpoints were oxidative and apoptotic, not inflammatory mediators in skin cells.
- **Replication.** Each key mechanistic claim rests on a single paper:
  - UVB/COX-2/MAPK/NF-κB/Nrf2 in keratinocytes: Sci Rep 2025.
  - TrxR induction: Machała 2022.
  - Mast-cell inhibition: Persia 2014.
  - PGE2 in keratinocytes: CIMB 2026, which is industry-affiliated.
  - The one independent olive-leaf/UVA fibroblast study (Czerwińska 2016) did not reproduce an ROS benefit.
- **Stressors.** Most skin-cell work uses UVA, H2O2 or bacteria rather than UVB, the main driver of sunburn erythema. Endpoints are mostly oxidative stress and viability rather than the mediators that dilate skin vessels (PGE2, NO, histamine, CGRP, VEGF).

### Gaps
- **Not found:**
  - Oleuropein or OLE in NHEK under UVB.
  - Any olive-leaf study in SZ95 sebocytes.
  - Studies in skin-derived microvascular endothelial cells (HDMEC/HMEC-1).
  - Human skin mast cells (e.g., LAD2 or skin-derived).
  - Hs68 studies other than Machała 2022.
  - Oleuropein effects on MMP-1/collagen **protein** under UVB, other than Sci Rep 2025, which found no single-compound protein effect.
  - Ex vivo human skin explants with UV and olive leaf/oleuropein (a targeted search returned none).
- **Unavailable numbers (full texts blocked):** fold-changes and p-values for Sci Rep 2025, Machała 2022 and Persia 2014; HO-1/MDA/SOD data in Sci Rep 2025; CIMB 2026 concentrations and OLE composition.
- **Funding statements could not be checked** for any paper. The only confirmed industry link is the Durae Corporation authorship of CIMB 2026.
- A 2023 Molecules paper (Italian group) on OLE-loaded facial-mask films tested against UVA in fibroblasts appeared only as a title-level hit and was not verified.

## Key Question 2 — Rosacea-relevant targets (LL-37/cathelicidin, KLK5, TLR2, TRPV1, mast cells, MMP-9, VEGF) and rosacea models

### Takeaway
**No study of olive leaf extract, oleuropein or hydroxytyrosol in any rosacea model was found, including the LL-37-induced rosacea-like mouse model.** No data were found on LL-37/cathelicidin, KLK5, TLR2, functional TRPV1, CGRP or substance P for these compounds in skin. Adjacent evidence exists only for:
- mast-cell degranulation (rat peritoneal cells, 10–400 µM);
- MMP-9 and COX-2 in endothelial cells;
- TLR4 rather than TLR2 (macrophages);
- VEGF, which oleuropein lowered in chronically UVB-exposed mouse skin but **raised** in wound and hair-follicle models.

### Cited Findings
- The LL-37 rosacea-like model is established. Intradermal LL-37 injected into Balb/c mice for 20 consecutive days produced lesions that expanded over the first 13 days and then plateaued, and long-term dosing gave lesions that did not fully recover. Searches combining this model with oleuropein, hydroxytyrosol or olive leaf returned no such study. — [PMC10136735](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10136735/)
- For comparison, rosacea-target data exist for other agents:
  - carvedilol reduced TLR2, KLK5 and cathelicidin in a rosacea-like mouse model;
  - azelaic acid inhibited KLK5 in keratinocytes and reduced KLK5, TLR2 and cathelicidin expression in mouse skin.
  No equivalent olive-compound data were found. — [Frontiers Immunol 2021 (carvedilol)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2021.609615/pdf); [EM-Consulte (azelaic acid)](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/823565)
- The only in vivo hydroxytyrosol inflammation study surfaced in this search was **systemic** LPS inflammation, not rosacea. Balb/c mice pretreated with HT 40 and 80 mg/kg body weight showed suppressed up-regulation of COX-2. — [PMC6321432](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6321432/)
- **Mast cells:** HT and oleuropein (10–400 µM) inhibited degranulation of rat peritoneal mast cells induced by compound 48/80, concanavalin A and A23187. — [CONICET repository](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/handle/11336/32037); [IntechOpen chapter](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/66111)
- **Mast cells in skin, non-rosacea:** in a DNCB atopic-dermatitis model, Huang et al. examined skin mast-cell and eosinophil infiltration after topical oleuropein. The extracts confirm reduced IL-4/IL-5, COX-2, IgE, ear swelling and skin thickening, but give no mast-cell count values. — [APJAI article page](https://apjai-journal.org/?p=6874); [PDF](https://apjai-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/14_AP-200122-1309.pdf)
- **MMP-9:** oleuropein and HT (0.1–50 µM) reduced PMA-induced MMP-9 release, gelatinolytic activity and COX-2 in cultured endothelial cells. Oral OLE and oleuropein blunted UVB-induced MMP-9 in hairless-mouse skin. — [CNR record](https://explore.cnr.it/resource/item/396889); [PubMed 19776181](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19776181/)
- **VEGF and new vessels, anti-angiogenic direction:**
  - Chronic UVB (oral dosing, 30 weeks): VEGF and COX-2 rises were prevented, and so were increases in CD31-positive (endothelial) cells. — [PubMed 19776181](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19776181/)
  - Oleuropein inhibited HUVEC tube formation and lowered VEGF secretion in a melanoma model. — [PMC5458266](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5458266/)
- **VEGF, pro-angiogenic direction:**
  - In wounds, intradermal oleuropein 50 mg/kg increased VEGF protein (p<0.05). — [PMC3933436](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3933436/)
  - In telogen mouse skin, topical oleuropein (0.4 mg/mouse/day) up-regulated VEGF mRNA along with IGF-1, KGF and HGF. — [PMC4462586](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4462586/)
- **TLR:**
  - Oleuropein bound TLR4 and inhibited TLR4 dimerization and TLR4–MyD88 binding in LPS macrophages. — [Frontiers Pharmacol 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2021.617314/full)
  - Ryu 2015 concluded oleuropein acts by "inhibiting TLR and MAPK signaling" in RAW 264.7 cells. — [AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65ded5cf4c5aef494fde342e)
  - No TLR2-specific data were found. CIMB 2026 mentions TLR2/TLR4 only as background on C. acnes biology. — [MDPI PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/cimb/cimb-48-00549/article_deploy/cimb-48-00549.pdf)
- **TRPV1 and sensory nerves:** a rat antinociception study reported that oleuropein reduced capsaicin-induced nociception and the neurogenic phase of the formalin test. Docking predicted TRPV1 binding at −8.45 kcal/mol, which is a computational prediction, not a functional channel assay. No study measuring CGRP or substance P release, or skin flushing, was found. — [UNIMAS repository](https://ir.unimas.my/id/eprint/49586/)

### Inferences
- A rosacea-specific claim (e.g., "targets the rosacea cascade" or "calms LL-37/KLK5-driven redness") has **no preclinical support** in the retrieved literature. The most that can be said is generic in vitro anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activity plus mast-cell stabilization in non-skin rat cells at 10–400 µM.
- The VEGF evidence points in **both directions** depending on context: suppressed in chronic UVB damage, increased in wound repair and hair-follicle induction. An "anti-angiogenic / reduces visible vessels" narrative is therefore internally inconsistent with the same molecule's wound-healing and hair-growth literature.
- The minimal missing experiments for a rosacea-adjacent claim are:
  - topical oleuropein or OLE in the LL-37 mouse model, scoring erythema, CD31, mast cells and MMP-9;
  - a KLK5 enzymatic assay;
  - a TLR2-agonist (e.g., Pam3CSK4) keratinocyte assay;
  - TRPV1 calcium imaging and CGRP release from sensory neurons;
  - VEGF-stimulated tube formation in dermal microvascular endothelial cells.

### Gaps
- "Not found" results rest on WebSearch only (several differently worded queries on rosacea, LL-37, cathelicidin, KLK5, TLR2, TRPV1/TRPA1, CGRP and substance P). PubMed could not be queried directly.

## Key Question 3 — Animal skin studies: UV (oral and topical), wound healing, hair growth

### Takeaway
The UV animal evidence is **oral dosing from a single laboratory** (Kimura & Sumiyoshi, Ehime). Oleuropein was given at 10–85 mg/kg twice daily and OLE at 300–1000 mg/kg in hairless (chronic, 30 weeks) and C57BL/6J (acute) mice. Endpoints were skin thickness, elasticity, MMPs, VEGF, COX-2, Ki-67 and CD31; **erythema was not reported in the extracts**. **No topical OLE or oleuropein study on UV redness in rodents or pigs was found.** Wound-healing studies (intradermal oleuropein 50 mg/kg; 2–5% OLE ointments; 5% oleuropein cream) consistently report faster repair, but they are small, single-lab and use doses far above cosmetic levels. Only the burn study measured tissue oxidative stress.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Oral, not topical.** The Kimura/Sumiyoshi data are systemic exposures and do not show that a topical product reaches the same skin concentrations. Both papers come from one laboratory with no independent replication found, and neither reports erythema in the extracts.
- **Dose relevance.** The repair studies use doses far above a cosmetic leave-on: intradermal 50 mg/kg, which bypasses the barrier entirely, and 2–5% OLE or 5% oleuropein creams. They show biological activity but not efficacy at ≤0.1% oleuropein.
- **Quality.**
  - Small groups (e.g., 4 rats per time point in the burn study; 12 per group in Mehraein).
  - Randomization and blinding were mostly not reported in the extracts.
  - Comparators were vehicle or saline, and sometimes an active drug (SSD, Madecassol, Fucidin).
  - Wound outcomes and oxidative markers come mostly from single studies.
- **Context-dependent vascular effects.** VEGF up in wounds and hair follicles, but VEGF and CD31 down in chronic UVB skin, so oleuropein's vascular effect depends on context. This is important for any "reduces redness / visible vessels" messaging.

### Gaps
- **Not found:**
  - Topical OLE or oleuropein UVB erythema studies in rodents or pigs (no a*/colorimetry, minimal-erythema-dose shift or laser Doppler in animals).
  - Pig skin UV studies.
  - Erythema endpoints in the Kimura/Sumiyoshi papers.
- **Unverified in extracts:**
  - the oleuropein content of the OLE used by Kimura/Sumiyoshi and its supplier/funding;
  - Koca 2011 species and doses;
  - the topical dose in Huang 2025;
  - Mehraein young-mouse effect sizes, which are secondary only.
- The diabetic-ointment numbers' exact source is uncertain (strain inconsistency).

## Key Question 5 — Coherence check: what laboratory evidence would explain the lower laser-Doppler blood flow reported by Perugini et al. 2008?

### Takeaway
The laboratory evidence fits **reduced inflammation-driven vasodilation** best: less COX-2/prostanoid and iNOS/NO from inflamed cells, fewer pro-inflammatory cytokines, mast-cell stabilization, and, per the authors' own hypothesis, blocking of reactive nitrogen species. **Direct effects on vessel tone point the opposite way.** Olive leaf, oleuropein and HT are vasodilators and NO-enhancers in rat arteries, and oral oleuropein increased human skin perfusion in a thesis study. **Effects on new vessel formation** (lower VEGF and CD31) were seen only after 30 weeks of oral dosing and are unlikely to explain a short-term post-UVB blood-flow drop. No study has measured PGE2, NO, histamine or CGRP in UVB-exposed skin after topical oleuropein, so the link remains an inference.

### Cited Findings

**The human observation**
- **Perugini P, Vettor M, Genta I, Conti B, Pavanetto F (University of Pavia) et al., "Efficacy of oleuropein against UVB irradiation: preliminary evaluation," Int J Cosmet Sci 2008;30(2):113–120 (DOI 10.1111/j.1468-2494.2008.00424.x).**
  - Design: an oil-in-water emulsion and an emulgel containing oleuropein, with vitamin E as the reference. Barrier function, skin colour and microcirculation were measured non-invasively.
  - Two arms: the **protective** arm applied product **before** irradiation; the **lenitive** arm applied it after erythema induction.
  - Result: oleuropein formulations reduced **erythema by ~22%, TEWL by ~35% and blood flow by ~30%**. The protective effect was not significant, and the authors conclude oleuropein's soothing effect is stronger than its protective one.
  - — [Univ. Pavia IRIS](https://iris.unipv.it/handle/11571/140790)
- Perugini 2008 design and mechanism, per news coverage:
  - Subjects: **10 women aged 20–30**, with product applied 24 h after UVB.
  - Protective-arm redness: only slightly reduced and not significant.
  - Mechanism: the researchers think the effect may come from **blocking reactive nitrogen species**, while noting it is not fully understood.
  - Discrepancy: this article gives TEWL as −25%, vs −35% in the abstract.

- **The "0.1% oleuropein" concentration in the user's material could not be verified.** None of the retrieved abstracts, news reports or reviews state the formulation concentration. — [Cosmetics Design 2008](https://www.cosmeticsdesign.com/Article/2008/03/17/olive-extract-could-soothe-skin-after-uv-damage); [Univ. Mast-cell degranulation was inhibited (10–400 µM, rat cells). — [Nature (Sci Rep 2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-24845-4); [PMC9415354](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9415354/); [CONICET (Persia 2014)](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/handle/11336/32037)

**Barrier (TEWL) component**
- In humans, TEWL fell ~35% (abstract) in the Perugini lenitive arm. — [Univ. Pavia IRIS](https://iris.unipv.it/handle/11571/140790)
- HT microneedles raised filaggrin in UV mice. — [PMC12841782](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12841782/)
- In DNCB mice, an **oral** OLE + Spirodela polyrhiza combination restored filaggrin, SIRT1 and claudin-1; this cannot be attributed to OLE alone. — [KRIBB repository](https://oak.kribb.re.kr/handle/201005/24085)
- No direct oleuropein-alone barrier-protein (filaggrin/loricrin) or animal TEWL data were found. The keratinocyte proteomics paper's extracts did not report barrier proteins. — [PMC11751055](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11751055/)

### Inferences
**Ranking of explanations for the Perugini blood-flow reduction**

1. **Best supported: (c), less inflammation-driven vasodilation.**
   - Several independent cell and animal systems show COX-2, prostanoid, iNOS/NO, cytokine and mast-cell suppression at roughly 0.1–100 µM.
   - The Perugini authors' own mechanistic hypothesis (reactive-nitrogen-species blockade) fits here.
   - Background assumption, not verified in this research: UVB erythema involves prostaglandin- and NO-mediated vasodilation.
   - Caveats: the supporting data are mostly non-skin cells or non-UVB stimuli, one study contradicts the NO direction, and nothing was measured in human skin.
2. **Weakly relevant: (b), effects on new vessel formation.** These would bear on persistent redness or telangiectasia after chronic exposure, not on a short-term post-UVB blood-flow change. The direction also reverses in repair contexts (VEGF up in wounds and hair follicles).
3. **Not supported: (a), direct vasoconstriction.** No vasoconstrictor action was found. Every direct vascular study points to dilation or NO enhancement (aorta, uterine artery, pial arterioles, and oral human skin perfusion +25.6%). Any claim that oleuropein "tightens" or "constricts" facial vessels would contradict the available pharmacology.

**Mapping of laboratory evidence to redness mechanisms**

|---|---|---|---|

**What is missing to make the claim coherent**
- PGE2, NO metabolites, histamine and CGRP measured in UVB-exposed skin (animal or ex vivo human explant) after **topical** oleuropein at the claimed concentration.
- A dose-response with vehicle control.
- Laser-Doppler or colorimetric erythema in animals.
- Dermal microvascular endothelial-cell and arteriolar-tone assays (to exclude direct dilation at skin-achievable concentrations).
- A penetration study in Perugini's vehicle.
- Independent replication of Perugini.
- Confirmation of the actual oleuropein concentration used.

## Key Question 6 — Are the in vitro concentrations plausible in skin after applying a cosmetic? (from the objective)

### Takeaway
Plausibility is **uncertain and probably low for unformulated oleuropein**. It is a hydrophilic glycoside (MW 540.5) that the formulation literature describes as poorly skin-penetrating; HT is likewise described as having very low percutaneous absorption. The in vitro effects that matter for redness appeared at about 10–100 µM. A simple, assumption-laden calculation suggests a 0.1% leave-on could reach about 10–20 µM in viable skin only if a few percent of the applied dose penetrates intact. Concentrations of 100 µM or more (several mast-cell, keratinocyte and fibroblast studies) look implausible without special delivery systems. No study measuring oleuropein concentrations in skin tissue after a cosmetic application was found.

### Inferences
**Illustrative calculation.** The assumptions are mine and not sourced; the report writer should present this only as an order-of-magnitude check.
- Inputs:
  - 0.1% w/w oleuropein = 1 mg/g product.
  - Assumed application of 2 mg product/cm² gives **2 µg oleuropein/cm²**.
  - Assumed target compartment: the first ~100 µm of viable epidermis and papillary dermis, about 10 µL per cm².
- Resulting concentrations, if a fraction *f* of the applied dose reaches that compartment intact:
  - *f* = 100%: ~200 µg/mL ≈ **370 µM**.
  - *f* = 5%: ≈ **18 µM**.
  - *f* = 1%: ≈ **3.7 µM**.
  - *f* = 0.1%: ≈ **0.4 µM**.
- What it implies:
  - The 10–20 µM range at which pure compounds altered UVB-induced COX-2/MAPK/NF-κB in Sci Rep 2025 needs roughly ≥3–5% of the applied oleuropein to reach viable skin unhydrolyzed. This is uncertain for a poorly penetrating hydrophilic glycoside, and the El-Gogary "deposition" figure includes stratum corneum retention under an optimized microemulsion.
  - The ≥100 µM levels used in several studies would need near-complete delivery and are implausible for a 0.1% leave-on.
  - The calculation ignores dermal clearance, binding and metabolism, which would lower concentrations. It also ignores barrier disruption after UVB (raised TEWL), which might raise penetration in the Perugini setting.
- In vitro exposures are continuous incubations of naked cells, which further favours cell studies over skin.
- **Oral doses** (Kimura: 10–85 mg/kg twice daily) cannot be translated to topical exposure.
