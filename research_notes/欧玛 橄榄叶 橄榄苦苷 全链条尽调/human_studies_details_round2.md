# Round 2: unverified details of the two human skin studies on oleuropein / olive leaf extract (Wanitphakdeedecha 2020; Perugini 2008), trial registries, and the 2025 Frontiers oral RCT

Read this first: how the evidence was gathered and how verdicts are labelled.
- **Source of every extract.** All evidence comes from WebSearch result digests, meaning the search tool's own rendering of indexed page text. I ran 22 searches on 2026-10-24. No page was fetched directly, because fetching is blocked in this environment. I therefore did not read any PDF in full.
- **Quotation marks.** Text in quotation marks is the wording as the search tool rendered it. The tool sometimes paraphrases, so each quote is tagged:
  - **(near-verbatim)**: the rendering reads like source text. It was repeated identically across searches, or it carries source-style quirks such as "2 mo" or "evaluation were".
  - **(paraphrase)**: everything else.
- **Verdict labels.**
  - **VERIFIED-P**: found in a copy of the paper itself.
  - **VERIFIED-S**: found in a secondary source that summarises the paper, such as the CIR safety assessment.
  - **PARTIAL**: part of the claim was found.
  - **CONTRADICTED**: a source says otherwise.
  - **NOT FOUND**: searched for and not seen in any extract. This is not proof of absence.
- **Relation to round 1.** These notes build on round 1, `human_clinical_evidence.md`, in the same folder.

## 1. Wanitphakdeedecha R et al., J Cosmet Dermatol 2020;19(7):1662–1666, DOI 10.1111/jocd.13457 (SUPERHEAL™ O-Live Cream): population, dose, results, statistics, adverse events, funding, composition

### Takeaway
**Newly verified.** The Cosmetic Ingredient Review (CIR) safety assessment of *Olea europaea* (olive)-derived ingredients summarises this study and independently verifies four items from the user's material:
- all 36 participants were **female**;
- the dose was **0.6 g to the whole face twice daily for 2 months**;
- evaluations were at baseline, 1 and 2 months, plus 1 month after stopping;
- **16.7 % had mild, transient acneiform eruption**, with no serious adverse events. 16.7 % × 36 = 6, which matches the "6 of 36" figure.

**Still not found.** The following remained unverified even after searches for the exact numbers:
- every mean ± SD, including the Mexameter erythema series 245.6±52.8 → 243.9±47.0 → 240.4±50.0 → 236.4±49.6, TEWL 9.6±3.2 and hydration 41.7±11.3;
- the mean age and Fitzpatrick types;
- the statistical method;
- the funding and conflict-of-interest (COI) statements;
- who supplied the product, and its composition.

**Contradicted.** Round 1 found a lead that a review called the wrinkle change "not significant". That is a **misattribution**. The 2022 *Food & Function* review uses that wording for a squalene + C60 fullerene product, not for this cream.

### Inferences
- **How to cite the CIR details.** The CIR Expert Panel is a secondary source that summarised the full paper. Its sex, dose, schedule and AE details are probably faithful, but cite them as "per the CIR summary (2023)" until the primary PDF is read. The user's "36 women" and "~0.6 g" are now supported at that level.
- **Safety signal.** About 1 in 6 users developed acneiform eruptions; they were mild and transient. The study had no vehicle arm, so this cannot be attributed to the olive leaf extract rather than to occlusive or emollient vehicle components. It is still a concrete, citable safety signal that cuts against "suitable for all skin types" positioning and belongs in any safety section.
- **Vascular features, no redness benefit.** CIR lists **telangiectasia** among the photoaging features of the enrolled women. If the user's erythema numbers are correct (still unverified), the cream showed no measurable redness reduction in a population that included vascular photoaging signs.
- **Which formula was tested is still unknown.** Sibling notes in `formulations_international.md` §3 describe two formulas:
  - an older (≤2015) cream with vitamin C, vitamin A acetate, AHA/BHA and kojic acid;
  - the current cream with ceramide NP/NS and retinol.

  If the paper's discussion mentions ceramide, as the user's material says, that would point to the current formula. This is unverified. — [phyto-c.com, current Cream](https://www.phyto-c.com/products/superheal-o-live-cream-1); [Zenzone, older Cream listing](https://zenzonespa.net/product/phyto-c-superheal-o-live-cream/)
- **P = 1.000.** Round 1 inferred that P = 1.000 points to Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise comparisons. No new evidence for or against this emerged.

## 2. Perugini P et al., Int J Cosmet Sci 2008;30:113–120 (PMID 18377620): timing, site allocation, vitamin E arm, per-vehicle results, formulas, HPLC, funding, conclusions

### Takeaway
**Newly settled.** Extracts from the olivelife.gr PDF settle three items:
- each volunteer had **seven 1 cm² irradiated areas on the buttocks, two of them irradiated-untreated controls ("a, b")**;
- in the lenitive (soothing) protocol, measurements were taken **"before the treatment and 1 h after the formulations were applied"**;
- in the protective protocol, measurements were taken **"before and 24 h after the UV irradiation"**.

**Consistent with the user's P values, but not confirming them.** The extracts also say:
- the soothing effect was **significant for all parameters with the O/W emulsion**;
- the two vehicles **did not differ** in TEWL or erythema.

**Still not found:**
- an explicit 24 h interval between UVB and the lenitive application;
- the vitamin E concentration and vehicle;
- any vehicle-only site;
- the exact P values and the visual-score numbers;
- the formulas and the HPLC method;
- acknowledgements, funding and Lachifarma's role.

**Low-confidence new numbers.** One digest says vitamin E reduced the laser Doppler (LDF) readings by about 73 % and a* by about 58 %.

### Inferences
- **The seven-versus-five conflict reconciles exactly.** Seven areas can be split as follows:
  - 2 irradiated-untreated controls (a, b);
  - 2 oleuropein areas for the protective protocol (O/W emulsion and emulgel);
  - 2 oleuropein areas for the lenitive protocol (same two vehicles);
  - 1 vitamin E area.

  Round 1's garbled description of "two irradiated-untreated controls, two oleuropein sites and one vitamin E site" (five sites) would then be one protocol's sites plus the controls. This is a reconstruction, not a stated finding. If it is right:
  - there was **no vehicle-only area**;
  - vitamin E was tested in only one protocol.
- **Vehicle effect cannot be excluded.** The lenitive effect was measured **1 h after a single application**. An emollient O/W vehicle alone can lower TEWL and change a* over such a short window. With no vehicle-only area, the lenitive TEWL and a* reductions cannot be attributed to oleuropein.
- **Ratio to vitamin E (low-confidence numbers).** If the vitamin E figures are accurate:
  - vitamin E reduced a* by about 58 % against about 22 % for oleuropein, roughly 2.6×;
  - vitamin E reduced LDF by about 73 % against about 30 %, roughly 2.4×.

  These numbers come from a digest the tool itself flagged as partly reconstructed. Do not publish them without the PDF.
- **The two vehicle statements are compatible.** "Significant for all parameters only with the O/W emulsion" and "no difference between the vehicles" can both hold. The emulgel's own a* change could miss significance (the user's P = 0.128) while a direct emulsion-versus-emulgel comparison is also non-significant. This fits the user's per-vehicle pattern but does not verify it.
- **The protocols themselves are weak.** The authors rate the soothing effect above the protective effect, and they call the protective effect only "slight". Together these weaken any claim of "UV protection" for oleuropein in this model.

### Gaps
- **Items still missing:**
  - the vitamin E concentration and vehicle;
  - the exact per-vehicle P values and means ± SD;
  - the visual-score numbers and scale;
  - the full formulas and the HPLC conditions;
  - the acknowledgements, funding and Lachifarma's role;
  - the limitations paragraph;
  - whether the lenitive application was 24 h after UVB.
- **Why they are missing.** The later pages of the olivelife.gr PDF appear poorly indexed. Seven site-restricted searches with different vocabulary returned the same abstract- and methods-level fragments. The PDF, or the Wiley version, must be read directly.

## 3. Trial registries: any registered trial of topical olive leaf extract, oleuropein or hydroxytyrosol for skin, or of any Phyto-C product

- a topical olive **leaf** extract or oleuropein skin trial;

- **Not relevant.** NCT05310994, "Efficacy Evaluation of Wasabi Leaf Extract on Skin", surfaced in the searches. It is unrelated, though it shows the usual Mexameter MX18 setup for melanin and erythema index. — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05310994](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05310994)

### Inferences
- **The Brazilian trial is not leaf extract.** Its extract is olive fruit- or oil-derived and standardized to hydroxytyrosol, not leaf extract or oleuropein. It is the closest registered topical olive-polyphenol skin trial, but it is not evidence for Phyto-C's leaf-extract products.
- **The Siriraj study looks unregistered.** No record surfaced in TCTR or ClinicalTrials.gov. Many cosmetic pilot studies are not registered, but without a record there is no external check on outcome reporting. This matters here because the erythema index was a stated primary outcome yet no erythema result appears in the abstract.
- **The herpes labialis trial fits an evidence inventory, not a cosmetic claim.** It is an antiviral, mucocutaneous indication on the lip, not a cosmetic one. It should still be listed in any "human topical OLE trials" inventory.

### Gaps
- **Registries not queried directly.** TCTR, ChiCTR, ISRCTN, CTRI and the EU CTR could not be queried. A search naming all of these registries plus ReBEC surfaced no entries from them; ReBEC RBR-2mg3xw surfaced only in an earlier, broader search. Only search-engine-indexed records were seen.
- **Round-1 lead not re-checked.** The olive content of NCT05734703, "Skin Rejuvenation With Topical Extract and Other Nutraceuticals", is still unknown.
- **Herpes labialis trial details.** Its journal, n, product, concentration and registration were not verified. The ScienceDirect PII prefix S1550-8307 suggests the journal *EXPLORE*; that is an inference.
- **ReBEC trial details.** Its arms, n, outcomes and results were not verified.

## 4. The 2025 oral randomized trial in *Frontiers in Nutrition* (PMC12671197) in postmenopausal women with exploratory skin outcomes: design, n, dose, skin endpoints, results

### Takeaway
**Design.** A 12-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in **65 healthy postmenopausal women aged 45–70**. They took **250 mg/day olive leaf extract** (Solabia's Bonolive®, reportedly standardized to >40 % oleuropein) or placebo.

**Primary results.** The primary systemic tissue-aging markers showed **no significant effects after multiplicity correction**.

**Exploratory skin results.** A **subgroup of 26** (10 OLE, 16 placebo), assessed by video dermoscopy, showed:
- **lower pore number versus placebo at week 12 (p = 0.034)**;
- a non-significant skewness trend (p = 0.0847).

**Industry link and registration.** The trial is industry-linked: Solabia BV authors, and Solabia supplied the extract. It is registered as NCT05744453.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **The skin evidence is weak:**
  - the skin outcomes are labelled exploratory;
  - the subgroup is small (26) and unbalanced (10 vs 16), which suggests a non-randomized subset of the randomized cohort;
  - one endpoint reached nominal significance (p = 0.034), with no evident multiplicity adjustment across the skin parameters;
  - the trial is industry-linked.

  At most it generates a hypothesis that oral OLE changes pore counts.
- **No bearing on topical claims.** The trial says nothing about topical products such as Phyto-C's gel or cream, and nothing about erythema or redness.

### Gaps
- **Unverified wrinkle and redness claim.** One digest said secondary coverage reported that "wrinkles, redness, and other skin quality outcomes were not affected". Two later searches found no source mentioning wrinkles, redness, elasticity or hydration results. The full list of skin endpoints and their results is therefore **not verified**.
- **Other items not retrieved:**

  - how the 26 subgroup participants were selected;
  - verbatim confirmation of the ">40 % oleuropein" standardization;
  - whether the 2024 symptoms paper uses the same cohort.
