## Q5. Full text: specification (prior-art statements, extraction process, HPLC, compounds, compositions, stability)

### Takeaway
The specification sets out a multi-step process: enzyme inactivation; continuous extraction in a non-aqueous solvent, preferably chloroform, at 70 °C for 48–72 h; a water/boiling step; and an alcohol + activated charcoal boiling step, ending in a paste preferably containing about 6–10% oleuropein. It describes the HPLC conditions and names the main extract components. It contains **no** chromatograms, retention times or assay numbers, and its only stability statement concerns the vitamin C and C+E solutions, not the olive extract. Notably, the patent's own background says prior aqueous olive-leaf extracts typically contained 3%–18% oleuropein.

### Cited Findings
All quotes below are verbatim from [US 6,743,449 B2 PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US6743449.pdf), column numbers in brackets. "[sic]" marks errors in the original.
- Prior art, oleuropein levels (col. 2): "Previous olive-leaf extracts have generally been obtained using aqueous chromatographic procedures, resulting in a lower oleuropein content, due to residual degradative enzymes present in the aqueous extracts. A typical concentration of oleuropein in such extracts is about 3%–18%."
- Objects (col. 2): "It is therefore an object of the invention to provide a method for extraction of olive leaves that is nonaqueous and yields a high level of oleuropein. It is a further object of the invention to provide compositions containing this olive-leaf extract that are useful for skin treatment."
- Prior art, vitamin C (col. 1): "A stable topical ascorbic acid solution has been developed (U.S. Pat. No. 5,140,043; Pinnell et al., Dermatol. Surg. 27:137–142, 2001) … Related commercial products exist that provide a) 20% L-ascorbic acid, or b) 15% L-ascorbic acid, 2% zinc sulfate and 1% α-tocopherol (vitamin E) (SkinCeuticals, Inc., Dallas, Tex.)." Also: "This has allowed formulations of previous solutions of 15% L-ascorbic acid, 2% zinc sulfate and 1% α-tocopherol."
- Summary (col. 2): "The invention herein is a non-aqueous method of extracting olive leaves. The resulting extract is used, preferably together with vitamins C, E and A to form a skin-treatment product that has efficacy as a photoprotectant and antioxidant. The invention also includes methods for treatment of skin with the product containing olive-leaf extract."
- General approach (col. 3): "In general, the procedure for the isolation of the active ingredients according to the method of the invention in [sic] based on selective solvent extraction and avoiding exposure to any levels of heat that would degrade the extract. … and preferably utilizes chromatography to provide more rapid and accurate results."
- Four main steps (col. 3): "a) preparing the raw materials, including drying the raw materials; b) liberation of the secoiridod [sic] base by treating the dried materials with one or more suitable solvents to obtain the desired purity; c) extraction of the active fraction with an organic solvent or a mixture of organic solvents, and removal of the organic extract by filtration; and d) purification of the active fraction from the organic extract."
- Enzyme rationale (col. 3): "Since the active ingredients in olive plants are accompanied by certain enzymes capable of affecting hydrolysis of the active fraction, these enzymes must be inactivated before or during extraction of the active fraction. This is particularly important when fresh plant materials are used."
- Enzyme-inactivation options (cols. 3–4):
  - "a) drying the raw materials, for example, at 100° C. for 1–2 hours until the moisture content becomes below 1%, followed by slow drying at a low temperature as long as the moisture content is higher than 2%. … This step can take more than 24 hours in a low temperature oven (50–60° C.)"
  - "b) placing the dried plant material into boiling water or boiling alcohol for 30 minutes to inactivate the enzymes. Use of boiling alcohol is preferred over use of boiling water…"
  - "c) boiling either fresh or dry raw materials in acetone. This treatment is not preferred…"
  - "d) pre-treating the plant material with acid at pH 1–2 at cold temperature (preferably below 20° C., because the internal enzymes, such as β-glucosidase start to be active from 37–41° C. …)…"
  - "e) carrying out the initial extract at low temperature (10–15° C.) in the presence of magnesium sulfate; or"
  - "f) freeze-drying (lyophilization)."
- Preferred process (col. 4): "a) forming an initial extract of the plant material, comprising a treatment step selected from the group consisting of: (i) grinding olive leaves to a fine powder after they have been dried (preferably in a vacuum to eliminate the water content) in the presence of an anti-enzymatic compound; (ii) inactivating plant enzymes by boiling the plant material; and (iii) treating the plant material with acid at cold temperature; b) continuously extracting the initial extract with a non-aqueous organic solvent, filtering and concentrating to form a first paste; c) removing the non-aqueous solvent; d) suspending the first paste in water, boiling, filtering, and re-concentrating to form a second paste; and e) combining the second paste with alcohol and activated food-grade charcoal, boiling, filtering and re-concentrating to form the final extract. The final extract preferably contains about 6–10% oleuropein."
- Parameters (col. 4): "Preferably the anti-enzymatic compound is MgSO4 at a concentration of 0.1–1%, and most preferably at 0.25%. The non-aqueous solvent in step b) is preferably selected from the group consisting of petroleum ether, benzene, hexane, chloroform, and mixtures thereof. Most preferably the solvent is chloroform. The continuous extraction is preferably performed at 70° C. for 48 to 72 hours and includes maceration of the ground olive leaves. The solvent is removed by distillation in step c). In the preferred formulation, one part of the second paste is combined with two parts alcohol and one part activated food-grade charcoal. Each boiling step is performed for about two hours."
- In-process check (col. 4): "To check the level of the active ingredients (enzymes) in the extract, after each step of the extraction, the extract is dissolved in dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) at 5 mg/ml and filtered. The extraction procedure for each extraction step is carried out by adding enough organic solvent (generally one part dried plant materials to 4–5 parts solvent), and macerating the raw materials for 48–72 hours before filtering the extract and concentrating under vacuum, with sufficient extraction steps being done so that the final extract paste has 70–75% solids. … After a fourth extraction of the raw materials, in most cases, there is no remaining active ingredient in the extract."
- HPLC (col. 4): "The filtrate is injected into HPLC equipment (Hewlett Packard, series HP1100), with a stationary phase C18 Lichrospher 100 analytical column (250×4 mm). The flow rate is 1 ml/min and the absorbance changes are monitored at 280 nm. The mobile phase for the chromatographic analysis are: A. acetic acid: water (5:95%); B. acetonitrile, a linear gradient from 95% A and 5% B. The phenolic compounds in olive extracts are identified by their retention times and compared with their corresponding standards by UV spectra."
- Compounds (col. 4): "The main components of the extract of the invention are oleuropein, hydroxytyrosol, verbacoside [sic], triterpenoid B-sitosterol and 3 flavonoidal glycosides, the first two of which are the important ingredients in the extract, with the other components not affecting the performance of the extract."
- Stability (col. 5), the only stability data in the patent: "Formulations of 15% L-ascorbic acid (vitamin C), or 15% L-ascorbic acid plus 1% α-tocopherol (found in vitamin E) were found not to degrade after 6 months in glass containers." Also: "Addition of α-tocopherol to solutions containing L-ascorbic acid enhances color stability of solutions containing L-ascorbic acid. A concentration of about 1–2% α-tocopherol is preferred, since higher concentrations typically result in oily formulations, and because maximal skin absorption seems to occur at about 1%."
- Composition ranges (col. 5): "In the preferred product composition of the invention, there is at least about ½% of an olive extract, preferably utilizing the extracted olive leaves as described above; 5–25% L-ascorbic acid (vitamin C); ½–2% vitamin E component(s); and ½–2% vitamin A. Most preferably the formulation comprises 1% of the final olive-leaf extract of claim 1; 15–20% L-ascorbic acid; 1–2% vitamin E component; and 1% vitamin A. … The product may also contain 1–5% zinc sulfate. The final pH of the product is preferably about 3.0 to 3.5 but may be in the range of 2.0 to 4.5."
- Vehicle and most-preferred formula (col. 5): "The product composition in its preferred embodiment also comprises a vehicle which preferably comprises distilled water, alcohol, and the surfactant Brij 30 (made by ICI Americas Inc., located at Wilmington, Del.). Most preferably, the active ingredients in the vehicle are vitamin C (15%), vitamin E (1%), vitamin A (1%), and zinc sulfate (1%), plus olive extract (1%) obtained according to the invention herein."

### Inferences
- The "high level of oleuropein" goal is not borne out by the patent's own figures. The invention's extract preferably contains "about 6–10%" oleuropein, which falls inside the "about 3%–18%" range the patent attributes to prior aqueous extracts. The olive extract's novelty therefore rests on the process route, not on a demonstrated higher oleuropein content.
- The text conflicts with itself on heat. It says the method avoids "any levels of heat that would degrade the extract", yet prescribes continuous extraction "at 70° C. for 48 to 72 hours" and boiling steps of "about two hours" each. It also describes the process as "non-aqueous" while step d) suspends the paste in water and boils it.
- HPLC is used for analysis and quality control only, not for purification. The gradient endpoint, run time, particle size, retention times and any oleuropein assay result are not given, so the "6–10%" figure has no data shown behind it.
- The phrase "Most preferably the formulation comprises 1% of the final olive-leaf extract of claim 1" is left over from the application, where claim 1 was the extraction method. It shows the claims were renumbered and rewritten during prosecution (see Q7).
- None of the preferred formulas (15–20% L-AA + 1–2% E + 1% A ± 1% zinc sulfate + 1% olive, pH 3.0–3.5) is described as a commercial product.

### Gaps
- The patent gives no yield, no oleuropein assay data, no stability data on the olive extract or the full C+E+A+olive formula, and no solvent-residue data.

## Q6. Full text: Example 1 (Yorkshire pig "antioxidant protection factor") and Example 2 (sunburn cells), with figure values

### Takeaway
In shaved Yorkshire pigs, the full formula (20% L-ascorbic acid, 1% α-tocopherol, 1% retinol, 1% "olive leaf formulation") gave a 7-fold antioxidant protection factor (APF = treated MED ÷ untreated MED). Removing retinol or the olive extract gave 5-fold; removing α-tocopherol gave 4-fold. Without L-ascorbic acid the result was about 1, the same as the vehicle. The number of pigs, the statistics and the ablation-arm concentrations are not stated, and **olive extract alone was never tested**.

### Cited Findings
All quotes below are verbatim from columns 5–6 and Sheets 1–2 of [US 6,743,449 B2 PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US6743449.pdf):
- Protocol: "Yorkshire pigs were shaved 24 hours prior to exposure. 1.0 ml of antioxidant solution was applied daily to a 10×15 cm area of back skin for 4 days. A 1000 W xenon arc solar simulator was fitted with a dichroic mirror assembly with 1 mm WG 295 Schott selective UVB band-pass filter (295 nm). A fiber optic assembly delivered a 1 cm spot to the skin surface. An IL 1700 research radiometer with a UVA and UVB detector measured irradiance."
- MED determination: "On day 3, pigs received solar-simulated radiation at irradiance (UVB) of 5 mW/cm2 and fluences of 10–100 mJ/cm2 at 10 mJ/cm2 intervals to untreated skin to determine minimal erythema dose (MED). At this irradiance, there was about 40 mW/cm2 of UVA MED was determined 24 hours later, and was defined as the lowest dose that induces perceptible erythema with distinct borders." (The missing punctuation is in the original.)
- Challenge and endpoint: "On day 4, each 10×15 cm area received solar-simulated radiation at fluences of 1×–10× MED at 1.0 MED intervals. After 24 hours, erythema was determined visually. Antioxidant protection factor was calculated for each formulation as the ratio of MED in antioxidant-treated versus untreated (control) sites."
- Results: "A formulation containing 20% L-ascorbic acid, 1% α-tocopherol, 1% retinol (vitamin A) and 1% olive leaf formulation was tested for antioxidant protection factor. The complete formulation gave sevenfold protection as compared to the control. L-ascorbic acid was essential. Removing retinol or olive extract reduced the protection to five-fold, and removing α-tocopherol reduced the protection to 4-fold. Results are shown in FIG. 1."
- FIG. 1, "Antioxidant Protection Factor (APF)" bar chart (y-axis 0–9; the brief description calls it "a table"), read from the bars: **CEA Olive = 7; CE Olive = 5; CEA (no olive) = 5; CA Olive = 4; EA Olive ≈ 1; Vehicle ≈ 1**. Legend: "C-vitamin C; E-vitamin E; A-vitamin A; O or olive-olive extract; and the vehicle".
- Example 2 method: "Eight-mm punch biopsy specimens from antioxidant-treated and control skin receiving solar-simulated radiation at 1×–10×-MED as described in Example 1 were placed in 10% buffered formalin for routine processing and paraffin embedding. The tissue was stained with hematoxylin and eosin… Cells in the basal, spinous, and granular layers of the epidermis having pyknotic nucleus and hypereosinophilic, hyalinized cytoplasm were designated as sunburn cells. … enumerated by consensus opinion of two dermatopathologists viewing the slide simultaneously. The entire width of the specimen was viewed (approximately 16 high power fields) and the number of sunburn cells per linear mm of epidermis was calculated, not including hair follicles. It has been found that at levels above 35 sunburn cells/mm, that the damage is too great to distinguish sunburn cells precisely. Therefore, this level is used as the upper limit for calculations shown below. ### Inferences
- **What was novel, as the documents show it:** the claimed advance over SkinCeuticals' existing C and C+E products is adding vitamin A plus an olive-leaf extract made by enzyme inactivation and non-aqueous (preferably chloroform) extraction. The pig data show the olive extract raised APF from 5 (CEA) to 7 (CEA+Olive). The claimed oleuropein advantage of the extraction process is not supported by the patent's own numbers (6–10% vs. a stated prior-art range of 3–18%). The issued claims cover only the combination; the extraction method and the extract alone were not granted.
- **Relation to O-Live Gel, as a reading of the documents and not a legal opinion:** the INCI list has no L-ascorbic acid, no tocopherol or tocotrienol and no retinol or vitamin A, and uses zinc gluconate rather than claim 8's zinc sulfate. As listed, O-Live Gel therefore matches none of the claimed compositions. "Olea Europaea Leaf Extract" in the INCI says nothing about whether the extract is made by the patent's chloroform route. The patent's efficacy data (APF 7) apply only to the 20% L-AA / 1% α-tocopherol / 1% retinol / 1% olive formula, and the patent contains no data on olive extract without vitamin C (EA Olive ≈ vehicle in FIG. 1) or olive extract alone.
- **Ownership claims:** marketing statements that the patent is "held by" Omar or covers the "manufacturing process" are not supported by the patent face (assignee SkinCeuticals; product claims only). A later assignment to Omar cannot be ruled out because the assignment records were inaccessible.
