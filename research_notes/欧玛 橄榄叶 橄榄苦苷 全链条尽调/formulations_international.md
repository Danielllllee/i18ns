## 1. Current O-Live Gel and O-Live Cream INCI and official wording (verifying the prior material)

### Takeaway
The prior material checks out against search-index renderings of phyto-c.com (accessed 2026-10-24).

**O-Live Gel.** The official INCI has 11 ingredients, ending in phenoxyethanol, in exactly the order given in the prior material. The page calls the extract the "patented form of olive leaf extract rich in oleuropein".

**O-Live Cream.** The current Cream (60 g; Shopify handle `superheal-o-live-cream-1`) is a 17-ingredient O/W cream. It contains ceramide NP/NS, retinol, centella, aloe, green tea, grape seed and peppermint, and its only preservative-type ingredient is ethylhexylglycerin. The page uses the wording "Our advanced formula now includes…".

### Inferences
- **Gel.** The prior material's Gel INCI is confirmed in both content and order (subject to the access caveat).
- **Cream.** The prior material's Cream ingredient list is confirmed. The real list also contains four ingredients the prior material omitted: Caprylic/Capric Triglyceride, Cetearyl Alcohol, Cetearyl Glucoside and Ethylhexylglycerin.
- **Ceramide names.** Phyto-C uses "Ceramide NP/NS" in its INCI, "Ceramides II & III" in marketing copy, and SkinElite uses "Ceramide 3/2". These are presumably the same two ceramides under older and newer names. The mapping is inferred from how the names are paired; the standard INCI correspondence was not sourced in this session.
- **Nothing extra declared.** Neither the Gel nor the Cream list declares fragrance, a colorant, a chelator or a dedicated pH adjuster.
- **Cream and Lotion preservation.** The only preservative-type ingredient on either list is ethylhexylglycerin. Either the published lists are incomplete or the preservation system is unusual. This should be checked against a physical carton.

### Inferences
- **Lotion and Cream share a base.** The Lotion INCI equals the Cream INCI minus Centella Asiatica Extract and Retinol, plus Proline (listed right after B5), with the same order otherwise. The Lotion is effectively the retinol-free sibling of the Cream (inference from comparing the two lists).
- **"O-Live" is a naming family, not one technology.** The four product types share little beyond the olive leaf extract and the "SuperHeal" name:

  | Product | Formula type |
  |---|---|
  | Serum | L-ascorbic acid / retinol / tocopherol / kojic / arbutin serum (SD alcohol, laureth-4, zinc sulfate) |
  | Gel | Aqueous amino-acid / zinc / alpha-arbutin gel |
  | Cream, Lotion | Conventional O/W emulsions |
  | Mask | Clay / hydroxy-acid exfoliant |

- **"SuperHeal" is not limited to gentle formulas.** The line includes retinol (Serum, Cream, Mask), 15% L-ascorbic acid (Serum) and AHA/BHA (Mask). This matters when interpreting "post-procedure" positioning.

- the date of either switch;

### Inferences
**Gel. Premises:**
- (A) the brief's labeling rule: above 1% in descending order, the rest in any order;
- (B) alpha-arbutin is 2% (brand blog);
- (C) phenoxyethanol is ≤1% (the brief's EU cap).

**If A and B both hold:**
- Water ≥ olive leaf extract ≥ calcium pantothenate ≥ glycine ≥ zinc gluconate ≥ 2%.
- Glutamine, taurine, arginine and sodium hyaluronate are each ≤ 2%. They could all be ≤1%, in which case their order relative to one another means nothing.
- Phenoxyethanol is ≤1%, so being last tells us nothing about the others.
- Upper bound on olive leaf extract: OLE ≤ water, and OLE + water ≤ 100 − (≥8% from positions 3–6), so OLE ≤ about 46%. In practice the ordering cannot tell 2% from 40%.

**If A holds but B is false** (alpha-arbutin ≤1%): only the items above the unknown 1% line are ordered. Olive leaf extract could then be at or below 1%, if everything after water is ≤1%, or well above it. No lower bound survives.

**A tension to flag.** Under A and B, zinc gluconate and calcium pantothenate must each be ≥2%. If external use-level data (CIR, CosIng or suppliers) show such levels are atypical for a leave-on facial gel, then either the 2% claim is inaccurate or the label is not strictly in descending order.

**Extract mass versus oleuropein.**
- "Olive Leaf Extract" on a label means the extract as supplied. Oleuropein is some unknown fraction of it that the INCI cannot reveal.
- No solvent or carrier (glycerin, butylene glycol, alcohol) is declared. So the extract is either:
  - supplied in water, with its water merged into "Aqua" or counted within the extract;
  - a dry extract; or
  - supplied in a carrier that is not declared.
- Carrier-declaration conventions were not sourced in this session.

**Sodium hyaluronate (position 10)** is ≤2% under A and B. As the only gelling agent, it is probably well above trace level (hypothesis).

**Cream**
- **Olive leaf extract is probably multi-percent.** CCT (position 3) is the only liquid oil in a product sold as a cream, so it is almost certainly above 1%. Olive leaf extract (position 2) must then be ≥ CCT, which makes it plausibly a multi-percent ingredient. As with the Gel, this is the extract as supplied, not oleuropein.
- **Retinol and ceramides are almost certainly ≤1%.** For retinol (position 16) to exceed 1%, all 15 ingredients before it would each have to be above 1%, including both ceramides and every botanical.
- **Ethylhexylglycerin (last)** tells us nothing about the others.

**"10% olive leaf extract"** (if it appears in the prior material) is neither supported nor excluded by the ordering:
- Under A and B it is consistent, since 2% ≤ OLE ≤ about 46%.
- Under A alone it is unconstrained.
