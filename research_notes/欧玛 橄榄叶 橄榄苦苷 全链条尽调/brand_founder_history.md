## 1. Who is Mostafa M. Omar (degrees, positions, licence, publications; is he "Omar MM" of Lin JY et al., JAAD 2003?)

### Takeaway
University of Rhode Island (URI) records describe Mostafa M. Omar as:
- an Egyptian-born URI doctoral graduate (Ph.D. 1982);
- president of PhytoCeuticals, Inc.;
- a major College of Pharmacy donor who endowed the "Youngken-Omar Distinguished Chair in Natural Product Chemistry".

He is confirmed as the "Omar MM" of Lin JY et al., JAAD 2003. His listed affiliation there is **PhytoCeuticals, Elmwood Park, NJ**, not Duke and not SkinCeuticals. Phyto-C calls him URI's "Professor of Pharmacognosy" for "over 40 years". No URI record found supports that title.

## 2. Omar's actual role in the Duke/SkinCeuticals vitamin C → C+E → C E Ferulic line, and verdicts on brand claims

### Takeaway
The records show Omar as PhytoCeuticals' president and an outside collaborator:
- **co-author** of the 2001 L-ascorbic acid absorption paper and the 2003 vitamin C+E paper;
- **co-inventor with Pinnell** of a 2002 olive-leaf + C/E/A patent **assigned to SkinCeuticals, Inc.**

He was **not** an inventor on Duke's foundational stable-ascorbic-acid patent. He is **not** an author of the 2005 ferulic-acid paper. Trade press (2001, 2003) shows PhytoCeuticals supplied SkinCeuticals' vitamin C formulations under an exclusive contract, which PhytoCeuticals **ended in December 2003**.

On the brand's claims: "inventor of the first topical vitamin C" and "father of liquid L-ascorbic acid (NCI)" are unsupported. "Formulator and supplier for SkinCeuticals and a Canadian company (likely Cellex-C)" is broadly supported.
