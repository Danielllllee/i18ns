## 1. FDA warning letter of 18 Oct 2016: drug-claim wording, products named, adulteration findings, response, close-out, Form 483 and import alerts

### Takeaway
The letter (reference **17-NWJ-01**) is broader than the earlier pass recorded. On top of the drug claims for Phyto-C Signature Serum and the SUPERHEAL O-LIVE Serum and Lotion, it reached three more things:
- an olive-leaf product sold on the website as **"SUPERHEAL O-LIVE 1 Immune System Revitalizer"**, carrying infectious-disease claims (viruses, retroviruses, bacteria, protozoa; HIV, hepatitis B, tuberculosis, malaria and others);
- a **sunscreen** with prohibited "sweat proof" and "waterproof" claims and missing required warnings;
- a **plant-wide adulteration finding under section 601(c)** (insanitary conditions), covering "any other cosmetic products you manufacture".

The cancer claim on Signature Serum ("Relieves the symptoms of melanoma...reduces the risk of skin cancer") is now supported by two of three extracts. **No firm response, close-out letter, Form 483 text or import alert was found.**

### Inferences
- The "meningitis, Epstein-Barr virus ... HIV ... tuberculosis, malaria" list is the stock claim set of olive-leaf-extract supplement marketing. Reconciling the extracts, it most likely sat on the **SUPERHEAL O-LIVE 1 Immune System Revitalizer**. **Implication:** around 2016 the "SuperHeal O-Live" olive-leaf name covered an immune/anti-infective product as well as skin care.
  - Whether the Revitalizer was oral or topical is **unconfirmed**. Two points fit an oral supplement: the aggregator says the letter was issued by CFSAN and concerned "food and cosmetics", and TINA writes "skin care supplements". Neither proves it.
- **What section 601(c) means.** From background knowledge (statute text not retrieved here), FD&C Act §601(c) deems a cosmetic adulterated if it was "prepared, packed, or held under insanitary conditions" whereby it may have become contaminated. That explains why the finding covers the whole plant ("any other cosmetic products you manufacture") and needs no test result.
  - The visible observation is foreign-material exposure: ingredient containers left uncovered. The report should say FDA found **insanitary conditions under which products may have become contaminated**. It should **not** say FDA found contaminated or microbe-positive products, because no extract supports that.
- **The reference number.** "17-NWJ-01" fits FDA's district-office numbering (fiscal year 2017, New Jersey District, letter no. 1). This reading comes from FDA's naming convention, not from a source.
- **Timing.** The IPQ copy's title suggests the letter was public by 30 Dec 2016.
