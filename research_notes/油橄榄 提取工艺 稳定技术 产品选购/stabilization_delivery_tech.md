# Stabilization and skin-delivery technologies for oleuropein and hydroxytyrosol in skincare: measured evidence and commercial use

*Notes compiled 2026-10-25.*

**Reading guide.** Evidence tags: **[PR]** peer-reviewed paper (abstract, repository record or search excerpt seen, not full text, unless stated); **[SUP]** supplier or brand claim; **[PAT]** patent text; **[TRADE]** trade press; **[HEARSAY]** low-quality, secondary, advocacy or retailer page. Each bullet carries the year when the search output showed one; "undated" means no date was visible. Direct page fetches failed in this environment (WebFetch returned host-not-found for ncbi.nlm.nih.gov and cosmeticsandtoiletries.com, and was not retried), so every number below comes from abstracts, repository records and search-result excerpts; a figure seen only in a secondary source is flagged. Items already established in earlier research (El-Gogary 2021 deposition figures, Al Saqr 2022 28-fold flux, Cometa 2022, Perugini 2008 stability figures, Ghodrati 2026 pH 5.4, PMC8619189, logP and MW) are not repeated except where new detail is added.

## 1. Chemical modification: Inoveol OLEU (oleuropeinyl glucoside), hydroxytyrosol esters, oleuropein aglycone, peracetylated oleuropein

### Takeaway
Inoveol OLEU is the only chemically modified olive phenolic found as an identifiable commercial cosmetic ingredient, and every solubility and stability figure for it is a supplier statement with no method or dataset shown; the one efficacy number is an in vitro NF-kB result. Lipophilic hydroxytyrosol (HT) esters have abstract-level evidence of better skin permeation but remain research compounds or patent subject matter, and no skin data were found for oleuropein aglycone or peracetylated oleuropein.

### Cited Findings

#### Inoveol OLEU (INCI: Aqua (and) Oleuropeinyl Glucoside; Givaudan Active Beauty, launched by LibraGen)
- [SUP, undated] Identity and positioning: INCI "Aqua (and) Oleuropeinyl Glucoside"; "an hydro-boosted version of oleuropein"; activity listed as "Has anti-inflammatory properties, Protects again pollution" (sic); Ecocert/COSMOS approved, Halal, vegan suitable; Devereaux lists "Preservation: none"; suggested uses day cream, lip contour, serum, eye contour, hand cream, night cream; Givaudan's page lists targets including IL-8, MMP-9 and NF-kB — [Givaudan JP page](https://jp.givaudan.com/node/12236); [Devereaux Specialties](https://deverauxspecialties.com/ingredients/inoveol-oleu); [UL Prospector](https://www.ulprospector.com/en/asia/PersonalCare/Detail/831/213889/Inoveol-OLEU); [SpecialChem](https://www.specialchem.com/cosmetics/product/givaudan-inoveol-oleu)
- [SUP, undated] One of those listings (the search output did not say which) describes it as "higly stable, purified and shows excellent water solubility" (typo is in the source) — [UL Prospector](https://www.ulprospector.com/en/asia/PersonalCare/Detail/831/213889/Inoveol-OLEU)
- [TRADE/SUP, undated] Solubility and stability claims attributed to LibraGen in Cosmetics & Toiletries: enzymatic processing of leaf oleuropein; "same UV absorption" as natural oleuropein with "improved water solubility and stability"; "water solubility of > 100 g/L, whereas natural oleuropein has a water solubility of 3 g/L"; natural oleuropein degrades quickly with time while the derivative is more stable at "chemical and optical" levels (paraphrase in the search excerpt). No assay method, temperature, duration or percent-retention figure appears in the excerpts — [Cosmetics & Toiletries product article](https://www.cosmeticsandtoiletries.com/formulating/function/active/105370183.html); paywalled news item with the headline "Stable Oleuropein for Antioxidation, Anti-inflammatory Benefits in Skin Care" — [C&T news item](https://www.cosmeticsandtoiletries.com/cosmetic-ingredients/actives/news/21841809/stable-oleuropein-for-antioxidation-anti-inflammatory-benefits-in-skin-care)
- [TRADE/SUP, undated] Formulation guidance as relayed by the search tool from the same C&T article: use level 0.1-0.5%; supplied as a sterile 50 g/L aqueous solution with no solvents or preservatives; formulation pH below 6.0; add during cool-down below 30 C; homogenization and temperatures up to 40 C for short periods "do not affect the stability" of the active — [Cosmetics & Toiletries product article](https://www.cosmeticsandtoiletries.com/formulating/function/active/105370183.html)
- [TRADE, undated] CONFLICT on use level: a Polish trade article (Biotechnologia.pl) says OLEU is water-soluble, stable at pH below 6, with a recommended use level of 0.5 to 1% — [Biotechnologia.pl](https://biotechnologia.pl/kosmetologia/aktualnosci/inoveol-oleu-ekocertyfikowany-biotechnologicznie-pozyskiwany-surowiec-o-dzialaniu-przeciwstarzeniowym,1968). Givaudan's 2017 Active Beauty brochure gives dosages for other Inoveol grades (PHLO 1-3%, CAFA 0.1-0.5%) but has no OLEU entry, so the 0.1-0.5% figure may be confused with CAFA; UL Prospector's use-level field for OLEU is behind a sign-in — [Givaudan 2017 brochure (ecochem.com.co)](https://www.ecochem.com.co/pdf/Active%20Cosmetic%20Ingredients-%20Brochure.pdf); [UL Prospector](https://www.ulprospector.com/en/asia/PersonalCare/Detail/831/213889/Inoveol-OLEU)
- [TRADE/SUP, undated] The only test result found behind the product: "Tests utilizing 0.5% of the active inhibited nearly 47% of the Nf-kB inflammatory response of cultured cells induced with interleukin-1 beta"; this is an in vitro efficacy figure, not a stability or skin-uptake test — [Cosmetics & Toiletries product article](https://www.cosmeticsandtoiletries.com/formulating/function/active/105370183.html)
- [TRADE, 2009-01-15] Precedent for the chemistry: LibraGen's earlier polyphenol range was described as using "an enzyme controlled bioprocess" that modifies natural compounds "by grafting a sugar onto the molecule which results in water soluble, stable compounds" — [CosmeticsDesign-Europe](https://www.cosmeticsdesign-europe.com/Article/2009/01/15/LibraGen-launches-range-of-stable-polyphenols-for-cosmetics)
- [HEARSAY, undated] A fragrance and flavor database lists a synonym "product obtained by the reaction of oleuropein and glucose" and has almost no safety data — [The Good Scents Company](https://thegoodscentscompany.com/data/rw1758321.html)
- [PR, 2021, for contrast] Independent degradation kinetics exist only for natural oleuropein in leaf and aqueous extracts (first-order); none was found for oleuropeinyl glucoside — [Martinez-Navarro et al., Antioxidants](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8750354/)

#### Hydroxytyrosol esters and other lipophilic derivatives
- [PR, 2011] Procopio, Celia, Nardi, Oliverio, Paolino, Sindona (J Nat Prod), "Lipophilic hydroxytyrosol esters: fatty acid conjugates for potential topical administration": HT conjugates with fatty acids of different chain lengths were synthesized under mild conditions and tested across human stratum corneum and viable epidermis; permeation was linked to partition coefficient, molecular weight and isomeric configuration; the motive was that HT's three hydroxy groups limit topical use. Flux values were not in the retrieved text — [IRIS UniCZ record](https://www.iris.unicz.it/handle/20.500.12317/1997); [Univ. Chieti record](https://ricerca.unich.it/handle/11564/369758)
- [PR, 2015] Alonso et al. (J Pharm Pharmacol) measured porcine-skin absorption in Franz cells for gallic acid, HT and their hexanoate and octanoate esters ("antioxidant surfactants"); the excerpt states "The content of the cutaneously absorbed compound is higher for the antioxidant surfactants (ester derivatives)". Numbers were not retrieved — [Sigma-Aldrich tech-docs listing](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/AT/de/tech-docs/paper/635887)
- [PR, 2019] Hydroxytyrosyl oleate lowered ROS and malondialdehyde and changed skin-regeneration microRNAs (miR-21 and miR-29a raised, miR-34a unchanged) in human keratinocytes; cell data only, no permeation — [Antioxidants 2019, PMC6680967](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6680967/)
- [PR, 2019] Mild synthesis of lipophilic esters of tyrosol, homovanillyl alcohol and HT using acyl chlorides; the authors cite HT's hydrophilicity as restricting use in non-aqueous cosmetics — [Bernini et al., Antioxidants](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6617409/)
- [PAT, priority 2013-11-04; EP B1 published 2021-12-15] Eastman Chemical (inventors Boaz and Maleski), US9334229B2 / EP3066068B1: lipophilic HT carbonate esters prepared by a multi-step route, "useful as anti-aging compounds that resist oxidation", at 0.001 wt% or more in personal care products. This is patent text with no performance data in the excerpt, and no commercial product was identified — [Google Patents US9334229B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9334229B2/en); [EPO register EP3066068B1](https://data.epo.org/gpi/EP3066068B1)
- [PR, 2023, food emulsions only] A paper titled "Exploring the Use of Hydroxytyrosol and Some of Its Esters in Food-Grade Nanoemulsions: Establishing Connection between Structure and Efficiency" relates HT ester structure to antioxidant efficiency in food-grade (fish-oil) O/W nanoemulsions; this concerns oxidation of food oil, not skin — [Antioxidants 2023, PMC10669426](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10669426/)
- Hydroxytyrosyl acetate: no skin, topical or cosmetic study found; the only hit was a digestive-stability paper and a reagent catalogue listing — [UCM record](https://produccioncientifica.ucm.es/documentos/617c80479680f513054bcbc5?lang=en)

#### Oleuropein aglycone and peracetylated oleuropein
- [PR, 2018] Peracetylated oleuropein (OleAc) is described as a "safe, biologically active semi-synthetic stable derivative" proposed to make oleuropein more bioavailable and compatible with fatty foods; tested by enriching extra virgin olive oil and checking by LC-MS that acetyl groups stayed intact. The abstract gives no head-to-head degradation rates and no skin test — [AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65df8d5b0f3e94b9e5d9b453); [Food & Function 2018](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2018/fo/c8fo01874j)
- [PR, 2017] Lipophilic oleuropein aglycone derivatives made by transacetylation to offset the parent's hydrophilicity and degradability; permeation profile and antioxidant capacity assessed (ORAC, DPPH, ROS in SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells), with activity depending on lipophilicity; no skin model seen in the excerpt — [AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65df7b224c5aef494fe2d7e5); [Food & Function 2017](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/Content/ArticleLanding/2017/FO/C7FO01105A)

### Inferences
- Inoveol OLEU is positioned as a water-phase solubility and shelf-stability ingredient ("hydro-boosted"), not as a skin-penetration technology; nothing found shows deeper skin uptake. Because the modification attaches sugar to an already hydrophilic glycoside, a penetration gain would not be expected (inference from the chemistry described; no data).
- A consumer reading "stabilized oleuropein" is most likely meeting Inoveol OLEU; the supporting evidence is the supplier's solubility figure and an in vitro NF-kB result, with no public retention-over-time dataset.
- The HT ester literature points one way (more lipophilic conjugates reach or cross stratum corneum better than parent HT), but it rests on abstract-level statements, and the shelf-ready cosmetic link is missing: only a patent family was found.
- OleAc and aglycone derivatives are food or biochemical research tools so far; no cosmetic evidence exists to support a skincare claim built on them.

### Gaps
- Inoveol OLEU: no stability protocol or percent-retention data, no skin-uptake data, no human data, no independent study. Tried: "Inoveol OLEU" with clinical, in vivo, test; LibraGen oleuropein glucoside stability 45 C; supplier-site searches (Givaudan, SpecialChem, UL Prospector, Devereaux). The use-level conflict (0.1-0.5% vs 0.5-1%) is unresolved; the technical data sheet was not accessible.
- Publication date of the C&T article and of the OLEU launch was not shown; Givaudan's acquisition or ownership history of LibraGen was not found.
- Quantitative flux or percent-permeated values for HT esters (Procopio 2011, Alonso 2015) were not retrievable; no commercial HT-ester skincare ingredient was identified.
- Hydroxytyrosyl acetate and oleuropein aglycone in skin or cosmetics: not found (queries: "hydroxytyrosyl acetate" with skin, keratinocyte, topical, permeation, cosmetic; "oleuropein aglycone" and "peracetylated oleuropein" with stability and skin).

## 2. Carrier systems in cosmetics: measured improvement over a plain solution or plain cream

### Takeaway
Only two carriers have a measured skin-delivery gain for these molecules: the oleuropein microemulsion and the HT niosome gel (both established earlier); liposomes, nanoparticles, cyclodextrin complexes, phospholipid complexes and plant vesicles have formulation metrics (particle size, encapsulation efficiency, physical stability) or cell data but no skin-layer data versus a plain solution. Encapsulation efficiencies reported for olive phenolics in liposomes range from 34% to 76%, so a sizeable share of the active is not in the vesicle.

**Details and caveats by technology**
- Liposomes, HT: optimized conditions were 63 C, phospholipid-to-cholesterol 4.5:1, 5 mg HT and 6 mL Tween-80; HT liposomes also scavenged DPPH better than free HT. A secondary review states stability of encapsulated HT in water rose "from 7 to 30 days at 25 C"; this was not confirmed against the primary paper — [PR 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6152771/); [HEARSAY/secondary review](https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/history/show/54426)
- Liposomes, oleuropein: the soy-phosphatidylcholine study reported EE of 34% and delayed oleuropein degradation in pH 2.0 and 2.8 model drinks (a food setting) — [Food Biophysics 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11483-020-09650-y). In the 2025 three-system comparison, phospholipid composition governed which compounds were retained (non-polar triterpenes favoured over flavonoid glycosides) and one formulation (PG90) was most stable over 60 days — [Pharmaceuticals 2025, PMC12655330](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12655330/)
- Oleuropein versus HT loading in liposomes is inconsistent: a review cites 30% for oleuropein versus 12% for HT and 4% for tyrosol without naming the primary study — [HEARSAY/secondary, encyclopedia.pub](https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/22571)
- Cagliari vesicles: published in Eur J Pharm Biopharm 185:126-136 (2023); vesicles carried 20 mg/mL oleuropein and 75 mg/mL lentisk oil; only the abstract was seen, so whether free oleuropein was a comparator is not known — [Cagliari repository](https://iris.unica.it/handle/11584/374743)
- Ocular precedent: the cyclodextrin-plus-liposome stability result established earlier (PMC8619189) is an ocular dry-eye formulation paper, not a skin study — [PMC8619189](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8619189/)
- Cyclodextrins in cosmetics, generic: a review says cyclodextrin complexes "significantly" improve stability of substances sensitive to light, moisture and oxygen and can change skin absorption; this is not oleuropein-specific — [Aesthetic Cosmetology review, 2020](https://aestheticcosmetology.com/cyclodextrins-alternative-carriers-of-active-substances-in-cosmetics/)
- Phospholipid complexes: US8084067 (Indena SpA; inventors Giori and Franceschi; priority 2006-04-13; granted 2011-12-27) says the complexes raise plasma bioavailability through lipophilicity; Google lists the status as expired (stated as an assumption); the European counterpart EP1844785A1 is listed as withdrawn. No skin data and no cosmetic phytosome product were found — [Justia](https://patents.justia.com/patent/8084067); [Google Patents EP1844785A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP1844785)
- NLC: a separate NLC study in the same search results reported no oleuropein leakage over 90 days in aqueous suspension; the excerpt did not say which record, so candidates are listed and the attribution is unconfirmed — [UPV/EHU record](https://addi.ehu.es/handle/10810/43637?show=full); [UniCZ record](https://www.iris.unicz.it/handle/20.500.12317/91763); [PMC6952516](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6952516). HT oleate has also been used as the SLN matrix to carry oleuropein-type compounds, tested in antioxidant and neuroprotection assays, not skin — [UCL Discovery](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10163175)
- PCL nanocapsules: polydispersity stayed below 0.3 through 120 days; zeta potential shifted after about 60 days and pH drifted around 60-90 days; milky appearance unchanged. These are physical metrics, not HT assay results — [SciELO](https://www.scielo.br/j/babt/a/3GXvNPB9Cx8fFSPKnHxdwWr/); [thesis (UEPG)](https://tede2.uepg.br/jspui/bitstream/prefix/3342/1/Juliana%20Parente%20Meneses%20Ribeiro.pdf)
- Chitosan-BSA nanoparticles: early chitosan-only particles were unstable until a BSA complex was added, which raised EE and loading; fibroblast viability stayed above 93% and keratinocyte viability above 85%; authors call HT "extremely unstable" and frame the work as improving stability and skin bioavailability, with no skin permeation measured in the excerpt. A related Hyt@tgel (HT-chitosan nanoparticles in a thermo-responsive gel, 2022) targets osteoarthritis, not skin — [UNL record](https://run.unl.pt/entities/publication/1e5d72fa-2e18-4f7b-a2ff-6968af9a0097); [Antioxidants 2022, PMC9220116](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9220116/)
- Microencapsulation: search excerpts report that spray-dried olive leaf extract with maltodextrin or inulin kept more oleuropein through simulated digestion than unencapsulated extract, and that alginate released most of its oleuropein in the intestine; these are digestion studies, not shelf-life studies, and the excerpts did not identify the individual records (candidates: [UBU repository](https://riubu.ubu.es/handle/10259/9972); [UniTE repository](https://research.unite.it/handle/11575/111302)). A 2016 conference paper on thermostability of microencapsulated oleuropein had no retrievable data — [Marmara record](https://avesis.marmara.edu.tr/yayin/24ee816a-7148-41d1-b74a-e5cadb4ce8b8/thermostability-of-oleuropein-and-microencapsulated-oleuropein-determined-by-biological-antioxidant-assay)
- Hydrogels: in the 2024 Int J Pharm study (Feng et al., vol 661, article 124434) the Strat-M membrane discriminated between formulations while Bama pig skin did not, so the cream-versus-hydrogel ranking depends on the model; plain HT in either vehicle was a low-penetration benchmark (see also Cometa 2022, established) — [nufind catalogue record](https://nufind.nu.edu.sa/EdsRecord/cmedm,38972523)
- Plant-derived vesicles: olive wastewater and pomace vesicles were rich in phenolics and suggested as antioxidants for food or cosmetics (2024, Eur J Lipid Sci Technol); fruit-derived olive nanovesicles were studied as drug carriers (2024, J Nanobiotechnol); a general review covers plant vesicles in facial aesthetics — [HAL](https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-04701823); [PMC11575425](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11575425); [PMC9223821](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9223821/)
- Ethosomes and transfersomes, generic only: in a vitamin E and caffeine comparison, flux ranked transethosomes above ethosomes above transfersomes for vitamin E, while caffeine did not follow that order; ethosome drawbacks listed in reviews include irritation, instability and shorter shelf life. None of this involves HT or oleuropein — [Int J Nanomedicine 2015](https://www.dovepress.com/development-characterization-and-skin-delivery-studies-of-related-ultr-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-IJN); [Transethosome review](https://researcher.manipal.edu/en/publications/transethosomes-a-comprehensive-review-of-ultra-deformable-vesicul/)

### Inferences
- A label saying "liposomal", "nano-encapsulated", "ethosomal" or "phytosome" on an olive-phenolic skincare product is not backed by skin-delivery data for that carrier; the best-supported carriers for these molecules (microemulsion for oleuropein, niosome gel for HT) have not been found in any commercial product.
- Because liposome EE for olive phenolics in the sources is 34-76% (oleuropein) and 45% (HT), roughly a quarter to two thirds of the active would sit outside the vesicle in those preparations (arithmetic on the reported EE values, not a measured outside fraction).
- Several papers report "stability" as particle size, PDI, zeta potential, pH or appearance rather than assayed oleuropein or HT, so physical stability of a carrier should not be read as retention of the active (observation from the abstracts and excerpts seen: Kesente 2017, PCL nanocapsules 2023).
- For cosmetic matrices, the only encapsulation-versus-pure-extract stability comparison found (PLA nanoparticles, 3 months) judged the finished product by viscosity, pH, odor and phase behaviour, not by oleuropein content.

### Gaps
- Skin-layer deposition or permeation versus a plain solution or cream for liposomes, SLN, NLC, ethosomes, transfersomes, cyclodextrin complexes, phytosomes, plant vesicles and PLA, PCL or chitosan nanoparticles carrying oleuropein or HT: not found. Queries tried include oleuropein or HT with liposome, phytosome, phospholipid complex, ethosome, transfersome, transethosome, deformable liposomes, SLN, NLC, nanoemulsion, Franz cell, skin permeation, plus supplier-domain searches.
- Numerical stability results for the cyclodextrin complexes (percent oleuropein remaining under light, heat, humidity) and the Feng 2024 stability tables were not retrievable from abstracts.
- Whether the Cagliari 2023 vesicles beat free oleuropein is unknown from the abstract.
- A 2026 result reporting Eudragit-coated liposome entrapment of 65% (oleuropein) and 96% (HT) appeared in a search excerpt but the page could not be pinned to a URL, so it is not used as evidence.

## 3. Vehicle and packaging: pH, anhydrous formats, antioxidants and chelators, airless or opaque packs

### Takeaway
Measured retention data in finished cosmetics is thin: acid pH (2.0) accelerates oleuropein hydrolysis in aqueous extract, formulators converge on pH about 5-6 with cool-down addition, and the only finished-formula antioxidant evidence is that 3-O-ethyl-L-ascorbic acid prevented HT discoloration in storage. No measured oleuropein or HT retention was found for anhydrous formats, chelators, or airless or opaque packaging.

### Cited Findings
- [PR, 2021] Aqueous olive leaf extract solutions studied at 5-90 C and pH 2-6: oleuropein degradation followed first-order kinetics, was fastest at pH 2.0, and was about 100 times slower at higher pH in the range tested; HT formed by hydrolysis rose at a zero-order rate at a given pH. Olive leaf powder kept at 57% relative humidity or higher was more stable after six months. Nothing above pH 6 was tested in that paper — [Martinez-Navarro et al., Antioxidants 2021, PMC8750354](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8750354/)
- [PR, 2026] The Ankara University paper on oleuropein ointment, cream and gel (cream F2.1 best, pH about 5.4) appears to be the Ghodrati 2026 result established earlier (author names were not shown in the search output); additional detail: ointment, cream and gel were made at 1%, 2.5% and 5% oleuropein, with "acceptable active retention under standard conditions" and "partial degradation" under accelerated storage (one search output mislabelled this as a 2023-era study; the journal listing is Pharm Dev Technol vol 31, no 4, 2026) — [PubMed 42052675](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42052675/); [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10837450.2026.2666098)
- [SUP/TRADE, undated] Supplier guidance for Inoveol OLEU: pH below 6.0 and addition below 30 C during cool-down — [Cosmetics & Toiletries product article](https://www.cosmeticsandtoiletries.com/formulating/function/active/105370183.html); [Biotechnologia.pl](https://biotechnologia.pl/kosmetologia/aktualnosci/inoveol-oleu-ekocertyfikowany-biotechnologicznie-pozyskiwany-surowiec-o-dzialaniu-przeciwstarzeniowym,1968)
- [PAT, grant date not retrieved] A patent-style example from the search for HT cosmetic stability (the top hit was US 11,896,708, HT plus sulforaphane; the excerpt did not name the patent) states that a polyphenol blend is "pH sensitive and temperature sensitive", should be post-added at no higher than pH 5.5 and below 40 C, and that an emulsion stayed consistent at 25 C and 45 C for 2 months and at 55 C for 1 month, with colour change at the higher temperatures. Assessment was by appearance and consistency, not assay — [US 11,896,708](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11896708)
- [PAT, undated] One patent on olive vegetation water adjusts to acid pH 1-5 (preferably 2-4) deliberately to convert oleuropein to HT, and another states HT "degradation increases at neutral and alkaline conditions giving unwanted products" — [US 7,261,909](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7261909); [US 8,968,811](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8968811)
- [PR, 2024] HT hydrogels and creams (Feng et al., Int J Pharm 661:124434): per the abstract, adding 3-O-ethyl-L-ascorbic acid "can effectively prevent discoloration" during prolonged high-temperature storage, and combining several antioxidants delays degradation most effectively. Stability figures were not in the abstract — [nufind catalogue record](https://nufind.nu.edu.sa/EdsRecord/cmedm,38972523)
- [PR, 2024] Olive leaf extracts stored at 25 C, 4 C and -20 C and heated at 70, 90 and 110 C: -20 C was best, 25 C was the least desirable for oleuropein and total phenols, and heating to 110 C was detrimental to oleuropein — [RSC listing d4fb00044g, Issue 3, 2024](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/fb/d4fb00044g)
- [PR, 2014, and thesis] Day creams with 0.1%, 0.4% and 1.0% olive leaf extract (standardised to oleuropein) stayed stable and homogeneous in appearance for three months at room temperature; an associated Palestinian thesis states oleuropein content in the cream fell significantly with higher temperature and longer storage and suggests a one-year expiry would still leave about 0.1 g per 100 g of cream (thesis method details not verified) — [Al-Quds, Al-Rimawi 2014](https://dspace.alquds.edu/handle/20.500.12213/811); [Al-Quds thesis](https://dspace.alquds.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/767209f6-45a0-458d-9f8a-3c19ae1807c3/content)
- [PR, light] UV-C irradiation of oleuropein standards produced fast decomposition to HT and elenolic acid, while oleuropein was relatively stable in plain water or ethanol; this is UV-C, not room light. The beta-cyclodextrin complex protected oleuropein better than HP-beta-CD against light, heat and humidity (see section 2) — [University of Bolzano record](https://bia.unibz.it/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Effect-of-light-irradiation-on-the/991005772981501241); [Liu et al., Molecules 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/1420-3049/27/16/5077)
- [SUP, undated] An ingredient-level storage claim: Gerbex Olive oleuropein ingredient lists a 2-year shelf life when stored below 30 C, sealed and away from light — [Knowde listing](https://knowde.com/stores/shanghai-jaka-biotech-co-ltd/products/gerbex-olive)
- [SUP, undated] Anhydrous or low-water formats are a brand claim rather than a measured one: Oliveda / Olive Tree People market "waterless" formulas, and the consumer-advocacy group Truth in Advertising reported that many products on the brand's site "contain water or ingredients that were obtained using water" (article undated in the output); the F83 serum ingredient list shows olive leaf cell extract, butylene glycol and ascorbic acid, with no antioxidant-retention data — [TINA.org](https://truthinadvertising.org/articles/olive-tree-people/); [Codecheck F83 listing](https://www.codecheck.info/p/kosmetik/gesichtspflege/general/oliveda-f83-ht-vitamin-c-serum-face.html)
- [SUP, undated] Marketing that HT is "significantly more stable than pure Vitamin C" appears on supplier pages with no data; SpringPharma's HTOrigin is described as having "excellent solubility and stability" — [Glabridin China listing](https://glabridinchina.com/product/hydroxytyrosol-olive-polyphenol/); [UL Prospector HTOrigin](https://www.ulprospector.com/en/na/PersonalCare/Detail/129988/8453153/HTOrigin-Hydroxytyrosol-40-Powder)

### Inferences
- The pH window quoted by three independent-looking sources (Ghodrati 5.4, Inoveol below 6, patent example no higher than 5.5) is about 5-6; the kinetic study shows hydrolysis speeding up toward pH 2, while patents describe HT degrading at neutral and alkaline pH, so 5-6 looks like a compromise window (inference; no finished-product study tests pH above 6).
- Cool-down addition (below about 30-40 C) is advised by the Inoveol guidance and the patent example, consistent with the heat sensitivity seen in extract storage studies.
- For HT, the only direct finished-formula evidence of a stabilizer effect is the ascorbic-acid derivative (colour protection), and no chelator (EDTA or similar) data exist in the sources found.
- "Airless" and "opaque" packaging are plausible measures given photodegradation of oleuropein under UV-C, but no measured oleuropein or HT retention in such packs was found, so any such claim is unquantified.

### Gaps
- Measured oleuropein or HT retention in finished cosmetics versus packaging (airless, opaque, clear), versus anhydrous or oil-based bases, and versus chelator addition: not found. Queries tried: hydroxytyrosol cosmetic emulsion stability pH antioxidants EDTA storage retention HPLC; Oliveda or Olive Tree People waterless airless packaging; oleuropein light degradation. One low-quality page in the results asserted that a serum in a dark airless pump degraded less than one in a clear jar over three months, with no source; it could not be tied to a URL and is not used (hearsay).
- Numbers from the Feng 2024 stability tests, the Ghodrati 2026 accelerated-storage retention, and the Perugini-type emulsion data beyond what is established were not retrievable.
- pH above 6 for oleuropein and HT in cosmetic bases: no data.

## 4. Human skin outcomes: did a stabilized or better-delivered form beat the plain form?

### Takeaway
No study was found in which a stabilized or carrier-delivered oleuropein or HT outperformed the plain form on a human skin outcome. The human studies that exist either used a non-olive comparator (clobetasol cream, vitamin E, untreated skin) or tested a plain extract, so none can show that the delivery technology added benefit.

### Cited Findings
- [PR, 2021] El-Gogary, Ragai, Moftah, Nasr (Expert Opin Drug Deliv 18(10):1523-1532): in psoriatic patients the oleuropein microemulsion outperformed Dermovate (marketed clobetasol propionate cream) on PASI reduction, dermoscopic imaging and morphometric lesion analysis; the abstract calls the clinical part "exploratory", and sample size, randomisation, blinding, follow-up length and whether any plain-oleuropein arm existed were not in the retrievable text — [Agya abstract page](https://agya.info/publications/books-articles/oleuropein-as-a-novel-topical-antipsoriatic-nutraceutical-formulation-in-microemulsion-nanocarrier-and-exploratory-clinical-appraisal); [Ain Shams record](https://research.asu.edu.eg/handle/123456789/228409)
- [PR, 2008] Perugini et al. (Int J Cosmet Sci), oleuropein emulsion and emulgel against UVB erythema with vitamin E as reference: the formulations showed a lenitive (soothing) effect, reducing erythema, transepidermal water loss and blood flow by about 22%, 35% and 30% (Cosmetics Design gives 25% for water loss, an unresolved discrepancy), while the protective effect was a "slight decrease in skin redness" that was not significant — [University of Pavia record](https://iris.unipv.it/handle/11571/140790); [CosmeticsDesign, 2008-03-17](https://www.cosmeticsdesign.com/Article/2008/03/17/Olive-extract-could-soothe-skin-after-UV-damage)
- [PR, 2024] Mayayo et al. (Cosmetics 11(6):198): olive pomace polyphenol extract with sh-oligopeptide-1 encapsulated in cellulose fibres by spray-drying, used in a cream; 30 healthy volunteers, randomised self-controlled design, paired t-test. Skin moisture rose on the treated forearm (p below 0.001), elasticity rose (p = 0.042), and TEWL fell after one week but with p = 0.099, which is not significant. The study is small and developer-run, the search summary says it had no placebo arm, and no unencapsulated-extract arm was described, so an encapsulated-versus-unencapsulated contrast was not made — [Cosmetics 2024 PDF (AINIA)](https://ainia.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/cosmetics-11-00198-v2.pdf); [Inves Biofarm](https://invesbiofarm.com/en/scientific-publication-in-cosmetics-journal-microencapsulation-cream-development-and-controlled-clinical-study-of-an-upcycled-polyphenolic-extract-combined-with-sh-oligopeptide-1/)
- [PR, about 2020 per search summary] Siriraj Hospital pilot of a plain olive leaf extract cream for facial rejuvenation: 36 participants, twice daily for two months; TEWL fell significantly (effect held one month after stopping) and hydration rose; no encapsulation arm — [Mahidol repository](https://repository.li.mahidol.ac.th/handle/123456789/56296)
- [registry, no results found] Brazilian trial RBR-2mg3xw is registered as a randomised comparison of a topical HT-containing formulation versus vehicle for facial dark spots or melasma; no published results found — [ReBEC registry](https://ensaiosclinicos.gov.br/rg/RBR-2mg3xw)
- [HEARSAY/preprint, 2026] A review-style preprint concludes mechanistic and preclinical support for HT in skin is strong but clinical evidence is limited and heterogeneous — [Preprints.org 202606.2056](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202606.2056)
- [SUP, 2025-07-03, not topical] The Nexira Oli-Ola result (12 weeks, 132 participants, placebo-controlled, elasticity and TEWL) is an unpublished industry announcement about oral supplementation and does not bear on topical delivery — [Nutritional Outlook](https://www.nutritionaloutlook.com/view/nexira-announces-the-results-of-an-anti-aging-clinical-study)
- Inoveol OLEU: no human study found; the figure available is the in vitro 47% NF-kB result at 0.5% (see section 1).

### Inferences
- The strongest "better delivery, human result" statement the literature supports is narrow: an oleuropein microemulsion performed better than a clobetasol cream in an exploratory psoriasis study; it does not show better than plain oleuropein, and it does not transfer to cosmetic claims such as wrinkles or hydration.
- Claims that "encapsulated" olive actives gave clinical improvements (for example the Inves Biofarm cream) rest on a 30-person, self-controlled, developer-run study with one non-significant barrier endpoint.

### Gaps
- A head-to-head human comparison of stabilized or carrier-delivered versus plain oleuropein or HT: not found. Queries tried: hydroxytyrosol or oleuropein topical randomised placebo-controlled trial wrinkles hydration; olive leaf extract encapsulated, liposomal or nanoemulsion cream human volunteers in vivo TEWL; "Inoveol OLEU" clinical.
- El-Gogary 2021 design details (arms, n, blinding) and whether Perugini's emulsion and emulgel differed in vivo: not retrievable.

### Inferences
- Mapping consumer terms to what exists: "stabilized oleuropein" most likely refers to Inoveol OLEU (supplier-only evidence; chemical modification by glycosylation); "liposomal" or "encapsulated" hydroxytyrosol most likely traces to EG Active's LD or LPD grades or to a generic lecithin liposome offered at 5-10% (supplier-only evidence); "waterless" is a brand format claim contested by TINA; "high bioavailability" for topical use has no direct support in anything found, and where used it relies on the supplier's own "bio-availability of the active in the skin" statement or on oral and molecular-size arguments.
- Given the gaps, the checkable items for a buyer are the oleuropein or HT content and form stated on the label, the assay method and storage conditions behind any "stable" claim, and whether a Franz-cell or tape-stripping study of the finished product exists (inference; none was found for any product here).
