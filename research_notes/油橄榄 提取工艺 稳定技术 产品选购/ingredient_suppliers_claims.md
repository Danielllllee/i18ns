# Branded olive-derived cosmetic actives sold to skincare manufacturers: supplier catalogue, claim wording, and evidence check (searched 2026-10-25)

Method, labels and scope:
- **Where the text comes from.** Every extract below comes from WebSearch result digests, meaning the search tool's own rendering of indexed page text. I ran 42 searches on 2026-10-25, in English, Chinese, Korean and Japanese. No page was opened directly, because page fetching is blocked in this environment. Text in quotation marks is the wording as the search tool rendered it. It is usually verbatim but can be a paraphrase.
- **Evidence tags.**
  - **[PR]**: peer-reviewed paper, seen at abstract or digest level only.
  - **[SUP]**: supplier or distributor statement.
  - **[BRAND]**: statement by a finished-product brand.
  - **[3P]**: third-party database, aggregator or trade press.
  - **[HS]**: hearsay. This covers unconfirmed or conflicting details, and digest details whose source page could not be pinned down.
- **Dates.** Each bullet gives the source's own date where known. Otherwise it says "undated, seen 2026-10-25".
- **Not repeated here.** These were established in earlier research per the brief: Deretil OE12S/OE35S, the Sabinsa Oleuropein 80 spec, Naturex 18 %, Voyageur 7.5 %, MySkinRecipes, Berkem R0570, Hallstar Eurol BT use levels, Lotioncrafter, Plamed PCR 40 %, Inoveol OLEU and Solabia Bonolive. Only new facts about them are added below.
- **Correction to the brief.** Every source found attributes OLEA VITAE to **Vytrus Biotech** (Barcelona area, Spain), with Intercare as the Korean listing name. No source connected OLEA VITAE to a company called "Vitalab". I did not search for "Vitalab" separately. See §1a.

## 1. Which suppliers sell branded olive-derived polyphenol or olive cell-culture actives to cosmetic manufacturers, and what is their identity and specification (trade name, INCI, process claim, stated active content, use level)?

### Takeaway
Beyond the commodity leaf extracts covered earlier, the branded olive actives found fall into four groups:
1. **Plant-cell-culture actives:** Vytrus OLEA VITAE / OLEA VITAE 02 (olive callus culture lysate) and Naolys "Fiber Booster Olive tree" (olive leaf cell extract).
2. **Hydroxytyrosol (HT).** These are either olive-derived (Genosa Hytolive, CreAgri Hidrox, BIONAP OLEA-HT 10) or synthetic/biosynthetic "nature-identical" (WACKER HTEssence, Viablife, Zhejiang Xizhenglin 浙江熙正霖, SpringPharma HT acetate).
3. **Fruit and leaf polyphenol extracts:** Indena/Kanebo Opextan, Inolex Phytotrace Olive, Shanghai Jaka Gerbex Olive, Shaanxi Huatai, Provital Olive Milk.
4. **Oleocanthal/oleacein,** found only inside a finished serum (Active Italia PureXerum), not as a raw material on sale.

Combined-name searches turned up no olive polyphenol active from Seppic, Symrise, BASF, Silab, Greentech, Mibelle, Lucas Meyer, Lipoid Kosmetik, Gattefossé, Euromed or Exxentia. Cell-culture actives state no marker-compound percentage. The HT suppliers do: WACKER ≥20 %, BIONAP 10–12 % HT plus tyrosol, CreAgri about 50 %.

### Inferences
- **Three supply chains for "hydroxytyrosol".** (a) Olive-derived products: Genosa uses physical-mechanical processing, CreAgri uses vegetation water, and BIONAP uses Sicilian oil by-products. (b) Synthetic "nature-identical" products: WACKER. (c) Biosynthetic/fermentation products: Viablife, Zhejiang Xizhenglin. All can carry the INCI "Hydroxytyrosol". A brand claiming "olive-derived hydroxytyrosol" therefore has to show supplier origin documents, because the INCI alone does not establish origin.
- **China's HT filing is not olive-derived.** The first reported Chinese new-raw-material filing for HT (国妆原备字20260085, May 2026) is for a biosynthetic material, not an olive extract. This matters for any China-market "油橄榄来源羟基酪醇" claim.
- **Cell-culture actives cannot be standardized by HPLC.** Neither Vytrus nor Naolys states a marker compound. Their quality is defined by process, not by an HPLC percentage, so "% oleuropein" standardization cannot be applied to them.
- **Most large actives houses do not list a branded olive polyphenol active.** In this round's searches, none of Seppic, Symrise, BASF, Silab, Greentech, Mibelle, Lucas Meyer, Lipoid, Gattefossé or Givaudan (beyond Inoveol OLEU and Naturex) showed one. Olive activity there is limited to adjacent chemistry: oleanolic acid in Sederma's Procapil, and Hallstar's olive-oil emulsifiers. This is an absence-of-evidence inference from a small number of combined-name searches, not proof.

### Gaps

## 2. What exactly do suppliers claim (efficacy, especially anti-redness, soothing, sensitive skin, post-procedure, barrier), what tests stand behind each claim, and how well supported is it?

### Takeaway
Only one branded olive ingredient, Vytrus's olive callus culture lysate (OLEA VITAE 02, at 2 %), has peer-reviewed, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled human data: two small trials (Cosmetics 2025; Processes 2026), detailed in §4. Only one of those measures redness: erythema after laser epilation, about 6 per arm (per one digest), over 14 days.

- **Supplier in vitro data:** BIONAP (ROS in keratinocytes); Vytrus ("mitochondrial synapses").

- **Nothing locatable:** Hallstar's 修红 claim, Gerbex, Phytotrace, Olive Milk, Naolys olive.

No supplier-run consumer or clinical study of sensitive skin, barrier (TEWL) or soothing was found for any olive leaf or HT raw material.

### Inferences

**Verdict scale (my judgment):**
- **L1:** peer-reviewed, randomized, placebo- or vehicle-controlled human study of the named ingredient at a stated dose.
- **L2:** peer-reviewed human study that is uncontrolled, non-randomized, or tests a multi-active finished product.
- **L3:** supplier's own in vitro/ex vivo data, summarized only.
- **L4:** claim with no locatable data.

| OLEA VITAE 02 (Vytrus), 2 % | Elasticity (R2), viscoelasticity (R6), fine lines (SEw) | Processes 2026 RCT, 3 arms, 60 days; n not found | **L1 design.** Moderately supported pending n, effect sizes and funding. |

- **Strength of redness and soothing claims.** For anti-redness, soothing, sensitive skin, barrier and post-procedure claims (the user's priority endpoints), no branded olive ingredient reaches L1 with adequate power. The best are:
  1. The OLEA VITAE 02 post-epilation RCT (L1, tiny).
  2. A non-randomized post-PDT study of an oleocanthal-enriched oil whose supplier is unknown (L2; §4).
  3. Older academic work on oleuropein covered in earlier notes (Perugini 2008), which is not tied to a current trade-name ingredient.
- **Standardization claims are verifiable only for HT and oleuropein powders.** The checkable ones are WACKER ≥20 % (TDS), BIONAP 10–12 % HT+tyrosol by HPLC, CreAgri about 50 % HT (trade press) and Sabinsa 80 % (earlier). They are absent for cell-culture actives and most liquid leaf extracts.

## 4. Supplier-linked studies in journals: OLEA VITAE 02 trials (Cosmetics 2025; Processes 2026), Nisticò et al. Medicina 2024, and a post-procedure oleocanthal study — who funded them, which ingredient or product, design and results

### Takeaway
- **The two OLEA VITAE 02 (2 %) trials.** Both are randomized, double-blind and placebo-controlled. The 2025 paper is by Gardiki, Varvaresou, Chaniotis and colleagues; affiliations were not retrieved. The 2026 paper's authors were not retrieved, so it is unknown whether the same group ran both. Both trials are small and short. The digests yielded no funding or conflict-of-interest statement, so a link to Vytrus could not be confirmed. Only the 2025 laser-epilation trial measured erythema.
- **Nisticò et al. 2024 (PureXerum, 1 % oleocanthal + oleacein, n = 70).** The paper declares no external funding and no conflicts. However, no retrieved text mentions a control arm. "Randomized" appears to refer to a single-product design stratified by sex, age and morphotype, and the manufacturer promotes the study on its own website.
- **Strongest post-procedure anti-inflammatory signal for an olive phenolic.** This is a 2019 Spanish non-randomized 47-patient study of an oleocanthal-enriched oil after photodynamic therapy. The supplier of the oleocanthal extract is not known.

### Cited Findings

#### 4.1 Gardiki V, Varvaresou A, Papageorgiou S, Protopapa E, Pavlou P, Rallis E, Papadopoulos A, Chaniotis D. "A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study Evaluating a Novel Skin Care Cream with Olea europaea Stem Cell Extract Following Nd:YAG 1064 nm Laser Epilation." *Cosmetics* 2025;12(1):2, DOI 10.3390/cosmetics12010002 [PR]

#### 4.4 Segura Palacios et al. "Topical treatment with oleocanthal extract in reducing inflammatory reactions after photodynamic therapy: a prospective quasi-experimental pilot study" (2019; PMID 30670258) [PR]
- **Journal.** The digests never named the journal. The ScienceDirect PII prefix S0965-2299 is, to my knowledge, the ISSN of *Complementary Therapies in Medicine*; this is unverified — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30670258/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0965229918308586); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/329598843_Topical_treatment_with_oleocanthal_extract_in_reducing_inflammatory_reactions_after_photodynamic_therapy_a_prospective_quasi-experimental_pilot_study).
- **Design.**

  - **Population:** 47 patients with actinic keratosis or field cancerization of the forehead or scalp.
  - **Allocation:** sequential and consecutive, not randomized. The first 24 patients received "an emollient oily fluid". The next 23 received "the same treatment pattern with an oily fluid enriched with OC extract".

- **Results:**

- **Not found:** the oleocanthal extract's supplier and concentration, the funding, and the inflammation scoring instrument.

### Inferences
- **Cosmetics 2025.** This is the only L1-design human evidence for an olive ingredient against **post-procedure erythema**. With about 6 per arm and a 14-day follow-up after a procedure whose erythema fades on its own, it can at most show feasibility. "Erythema activity decreased" in the digest does not establish a significant between-group difference; that needs checking in the full text. No funding or COI statement was retrievable, so "supplier-linked" is unverified rather than established. The ingredient is a branded Vytrus grade, and the trial used it at 2 %.
- **Processes 2026.** The design is better (three arms, 60 days, a flat placebo). Two points stand out:
  - The "polyphenolic complex" added no significant benefit over OLEA alone.
  - The endpoints are biomechanical and fine-line measures, not redness.

  The same ingredient grade appears in both papers. If the same group ran both, this is not independent replication. Authorship of the 2026 paper was not retrieved.
- **Nisticò 2024.** With no control arm, the −34 % to −52 % VISIA wrinkle changes over 30 days are pre–post changes. They cannot separate product effect from imaging variability, seasonal change, or the expectation bias that comes with single-blinding. Effects of this size from a 30-day topical serum would be unusual (inference). The "no external funding / no COI" declaration sits alongside the manufacturer promoting the study, and the product is a branded finished serum, not a raw material sold to manufacturers. The evidence grade is L2.
- **Segura Palacios 2019.** It has a vehicle comparator (an emollient oily fluid), which the other oleocanthal study lacks, and it targets a true post-procedure inflammatory model. Consecutive non-randomized cohorts, however, allow time-period confounding. It supports "an oleocanthal-enriched oil may reduce post-PDT inflammation" at L2, without tying the effect to any purchasable ingredient.
- **Overall.** None of the four studies measured sensitive-skin endpoints (stinging tests, self-perceived sensitivity scales) or barrier recovery after challenge. Only one, Cosmetics 2025, used an instrumental erythema index (Mexameter).

### Gaps
- **Cosmetics 2025:** full text not read, so the following are missing:
  - confirmed n and allocation method;
  - erythema and melanin values and between-group p-values;
  - funding, conflicts and acknowledgments (especially whether Vytrus supplied or funded the study);
  - author affiliations.
- **Processes 2026:**
  - n, authors and affiliations, funding and conflicts;
  - the composition and dose of the polyphenolic complex;
  - effect sizes;
  - adverse events.
- **Nisticò 2024:**
  - what was randomized (the unit and the arms);
  - whether any control existed;
  - the VISIA metric used (wrinkle score versus feature count);
  - adverse events;
  - whether Active Italia supplied the product or has links to the authors;
  - the actual registration number.
- **Segura Palacios 2019:** the oleocanthal source and concentration, funding, blinding, and the inflammation scale.
- **Plant stem cell review.** The review "Plant Stem Cells in Cosmetic Industry" (PMC11820651, 2025) surfaced in OLEA VITAE searches, but its content about OLEA VITAE was not retrieved — [PMC11820651](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11820651/).
