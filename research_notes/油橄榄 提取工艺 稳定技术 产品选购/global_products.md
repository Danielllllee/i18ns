# Olive-phenolic consumer skincare outside mainland China: products, ingredient disclosure, evidence, and a shortlist for redness-prone skin

_Method and conventions._
- **Retrieval.** Everything below was retrieved with WebSearch on **2026-10-25**. Direct page fetching is blocked in this environment, so every finding rests on search-engine extracts. These extracts summarise or quote indexed pages, which means wording, INCI order and prices must be re-checked on the live page before anything is published.
- **Dates.** Where an extract showed the page's own publication or update date, that date is given. Otherwise the page date is unknown and the item is dated by retrieval (2026-10-25).
- **Evidence tags:**
  - **[PR]** peer-reviewed;
  - **[BRAND]** brand page, brand press release, or brand claim repeated by a retailer;
  - **[RET]** retailer listing;
  - **[DB]** third-party ingredient database (Skinsort, Skincarisma, INCIDecoder, CosIng mirrors);
  - **[REV]** third-party review or blog;
  - **[USER]** consumer review (hearsay);
  - **[SUP]** raw-material supplier marketing;
  - **[EARLIER]** established in the earlier research folder `research_notes/欧玛 橄榄叶 橄榄苦苷 全链条尽调/` and not re-verified here.
- **Scope.** About 44 queries were run, in English, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, Italian, Greek and Chinese. Phyto-C, the Nisticò 2024 serum and OLEA VITAE 02 are included only as reference rows, as instructed.

## 1. Which brands build finished products around olive phenolics? (product table)

### Takeaway
Outside mainland China, few finished products make an olive phenolic the headline active. Those found:
- **Oliveda / Olive Tree People:** hydroxytyrosol on a base of "olive leaf cell" extract.
- **Beauty Thinkers:** hydroxytyrosol from an enzymatic EVOO process.
- **Fenolia and Prophenoil:** creams or oils built on hydroxytyrosol-rich extra-virgin olive oil.
- **Reference rows:** Phyto-C, and the Nisticò 1% oleocanthal/oleacein serum, which is research-only with no commercial product found.

Several better-known products contain olive leaf extract or oleuropein, but only as a minor or supporting ingredient: SkinCeuticals Phyto Corrective, Alastin Skin Nectar, Olive Manon, Nature Republic olive toners and Domus Olea Toscana.

Searches found no olive-phenolic-led face product from Korres, Apivita, L'Occitane, Kalliston or Olivie. "Olive Manon" turned out to be an Okayama (Ushimado) brand, not a Shodoshima one.

## 2. Which products disclose an amount or a lab assay? Is there any third-party or independent content test?

### Takeaway
- **No finished product disclosed a hydroxytyrosol, oleuropein or oleacein percentage**, apart from the research-only Nisticò serum (1%).
- **Partial numbers exist for a few products:**
  - Prophenoil: ">1,700 mg/kg total phenolics" in its single-estate EVOO (brand claim).
  - Fenolia: 15% or 25% EVOO "titrated in hydroxytyrosol", with no titre.
  - Nature Republic: one toner listing claims "10,000 ppm olive leaf extract" (≈1% of an extract of unknown strength).
- **No certificate of analysis or independent assay** of any finished product was found.
- **What does exist:**
  - a validated LC-UV method for hydroxytyrosol in cosmetic creams;
  - peer-reviewed evidence that commercial olive extracts vary roughly 38-fold in hydroxytyrosol.

### Cited Findings
- **Prophenoil [BRAND/RET].** Phenolic content "above 1,700 mg/kg" in the oil, which includes hydroxytyrosol and oleocanthal. No certificate of analysis was shown in the extract. — [olivaoliva](https://olivaoliva.com/en/prophenoil/12167-prophenoil-skin-care-jar-30-ml.html)
- **Fenolia [RET].**
  - Crema Eudermica 15: "Olio Extravergine di Oliva (15%) … titolato in Idrossitirosolo", with no titre given.
  - Oleocrema Eutrofica 25: 25% EVOO plus hydroxytyrosol from olive extract "(elaVida®)", with no amount.

  — [1000farmacie](https://www.1000farmacie.it/fenolia-crema-eudermica-15-100-ml.html); [TopFarmacia](https://www.topfarmacia.it/p-fenolia-oleocrema-eutrofica-50ml-25-dosaggio)
- **Nature Republic [RET].** "Real Real Moisture Olive Toner" is said to contain 10,000 ppm olive leaf extract. — [IBSPOT](https://ibspot.com/products/nature-republic-nature-republic-real-real-moisture-olive-toner-1081)
- **Oliveda [REV].** "Standardized olive extracts rich in hydroxytyrosol", with no figure. Illuminate Labs says the Oliveda product it "analyzed did not contain hydroxytyrosol", without naming the product or the method. — [Olivea review](https://www.myolivea.com/blogs/news/oliveda-review); [Illuminate Labs](https://illuminatelabs.org/en-in/blogs/health/oliveda-skincare-review)
- **ORAC figures [REV, brand-affiliated].** Brand-affiliated sources cite ORAC values for hydroxytyrosol of about 27,000 (pro-Oliveda review) and 40,000 µmol TE/g (Olivea blog). These are marketing numbers and not product assays. — [Olivea review](https://www.myolivea.com/blogs/news/oliveda-review); [Olivea hydroxytyrosol facts](https://myolivea.com/blogs/news/hydroxytyrosol-facts)
- **SkinCeuticals, Alastin, Beauty Thinkers, Olive Manon, Shodoshima, Domus.** No amount disclosed in any extract (see cards P2, P3, P7–P12).
- **Validated assay method [PR].** Miralles et al., *J Pharm Biomed Anal* 2015;102:157–161 (DOI 10.1016/j.jpba.2014.09.016), validated an LC-UV method for hydroxytyrosol and tyrosol "in olive extracts and cosmetic creams":
  - C18 column at 35 °C;
  - isocratic ethanol / 1% acetic acid at pH 5 (5:95 v/v), 1 mL/min;
  - detection at 280 nm;
  - recoveries 95–107%, repeatability 1.1–3.6%, detection limits below µg/mL.

  — [Sigma-Aldrich tech-doc listing](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/AT/en/tech-docs/paper/525417); [Universitat de València record](https://producciocientifica.uv.es/documentos/5ed320ae2999526aa7412caa?lang=de)
- **Other standards and methods.**
  - ISO 23942:2022 (formerly ISO/TS 23942:2020) covers hydroxytyrosol and tyrosol by RP-HPLC in extra-virgin olive oil only. — [iTeh ISO 23942:2022](https://iteh.es/catalog/standards/iso/fa9b6692-07c6-4d9e-86e3-82a0fb870b67/iso-23942-2022)
  - A plasma HPLC-fluorescence assay quantifies oleuropein (LOQ 3 µg/mL) and hydroxytyrosol (LOQ 30 ng/mL). No cream-specific oleuropein method was found. — [PubMed 12535851](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12535851)
- **Raw-extract variability [PR].** A peer-reviewed analysis of 10 commercial extracts found hydroxytyrosol ranging from about **163 to 6,216 ppm**. The extract also notes that hydroxytyrosol in olive leaves is generally low and depends on agricultural factors. The search result did not make clear which of these two articles holds which statement. — [PMC10818913](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10818913); [PMC7570885](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7570885)
- **Formulation use levels [academic repository].** Olive-leaf-extract day creams at 0.1%, 0.4% and 1.0% matched a commercial cream's physical properties and stayed stable for months at room temperature. — [Al-Quds University repository](https://dspace.alquds.edu/items/954ffc8e-01e8-4908-bb32-56694374c51b/full)
- **Supplier standardisation [SUP].** Supplier grades are sold at defined hydroxytyrosol levels, e.g. Naturolive HT20 at 20%. The INCI name on a consumer label does not reveal which grade was used. — [SpecialChem](https://www.specialchem.com/cosmetics/product/deretil-nature-naturolive-ht20)

### Inferences
- **Labels carry no dose information.** Commercial extracts vary about 38-fold in hydroxytyrosol (163–6,216 ppm), and supplier grades range up to 20% hydroxytyrosol. So "Olea Europaea Leaf Extract" on a label says nothing about phenolic dose. Only a certificate of analysis or an independent assay can.
- **Prophenoil's number is the only one tied to a product.** If the jar is neat EVOO, >1,700 mg/kg equals ≥0.17% total phenolics. That is total phenolics, not hydroxytyrosol alone, and it is unverified.
- **Fenolia's dose cannot be estimated.** Its "15% EVOO titrated in HT" gives no titre.
- **An independent test is feasible.** The Miralles 2015 method was designed for creams, so a hydroxytyrosol content check by a contract lab is technically straightforward. It has simply not been published for any named product found here.

### Gaps
- No certificate of analysis, third-party lab report, consumer-group test (e.g. Öko-Test or Stiftung Warentest) or academic market survey was found measuring olive phenolics in named finished products. Searches tried: "hydroxytyrosol oleuropein quantification commercial cosmetic creams HPLC analysis" and "Oliveda dermatest clinical study". Consumer-test magazines were not searched separately because of the budget.
- Which hydroxytyrosol grade Oliveda, Beauty Thinkers, Fenolia (elaVida®) and Domus use is unknown.

## 3. Product-level clinical or consumer tests (redness, sensitive skin, barrier), and ranking by transparency and evidence

### Takeaway
- **Redness-specific data is thin.** The only product-level data on a redness population is SkinCeuticals' own test of Phyto Corrective Gel: n=30, a single application, an infrared skin-temperature drop of −5% immediately after use, and no reported redness score. Its barrier figure comes from an in-vitro 3D model. The olive leaf extract is a minor ingredient and the gel contains fragrance and eucalyptus oil.
- **No phenolic-led product has a published test.** None of Oliveda, Beauty Thinkers, Fenolia or Prophenoil has a published human test. Beauty Thinkers' "clinically proven" barrier claims come without a design.
- **The best olive-phenolic human data is on unsold or comparison products:** the Nisticò 1% serum (single-blind RCT, wrinkles only) and Phyto-C Cream (open-label n=36, no verified redness benefit) [EARLIER].
- **Oral data with skin endpoints exists for two ingredients:** Oli-Ola (placebo-controlled, not peer-reviewed) and Bonolive (exploratory skin subgroup).

### Inferences

#### Ranking by transparency and evidence (not by efficacy)
Scoring heuristic used for the ranking:
- **Transparency:** is the olive active named and placed in a public INCI; is an amount disclosed; is there an assay.
- **Evidence:** is there a product-level human test; how relevant is it to redness or barrier; is it independent.
- **Redness lens:** penalise fragrance, essential oils, high ethanol and retinoids.

#### Shortlist a redness-prone consumer could reasonably try (inference; patch-test everything)
1. **Beauty Thinkers Hydrator 3 Barrier Support** (€68–69 / 30 ml). The concept fits best: claimed fragrance-free, built on ceramides and phytosphingosine, hydroxytyrosol named. Confirm the full INCI on pack before buying.
2. **Fenolia Crema Eudermica 15** (100 ml, Italian pharmacies). Claimed fragrance- and preservative-free, formulated for delicate skin, olive phenolics via hydroxytyrosol-titrated EVOO. It contains several plant extracts and an oil-rich base, so confirm the INCI.
3. **SkinCeuticals Phyto Corrective Gel.** It has the only redness-population (thermal) data and is readily available to Chinese buyers. But it contains parfum and eucalyptus leaf oil, and the olive extract is minor, so it suits only consumers who already tolerate fragranced products.
4. **Phyto-C O-Live Gel** (comparison benchmark). Lowest irritant load of the olive-leaf products, but the most expensive per ml among the comparators, and there is no redness data.

**Lower priority:**
- Oliveda F59: fragrance components, no data, MLM channel.
- Prophenoil: neat EVOO, so tolerability on barrier-impaired skin is unknown.
- Oleocrema 25: vitamin A.
- Domus Olea Toscana night cream: lavender water.
- Olive Manon whitening lotion: high ethanol.

### Gaps
- **SkinCeuticals test details.** Whether the n=30 study had a comparator or blinding, who ran it, and whether visible redness (a\* or erythema index) was measured: not found in retailer footnotes. The SkinCeuticals site was not reachable.
- **Beauty Thinkers.** The "clinically proven" barrier test (design, n, lab) was not found.
- **Missing tests.** No product-level redness, TEWL or erythema data was found for Oliveda, Fenolia, Prophenoil, Alastin (olive-specific), Olive Manon, Shodoshima, Nature Republic or Domus.
